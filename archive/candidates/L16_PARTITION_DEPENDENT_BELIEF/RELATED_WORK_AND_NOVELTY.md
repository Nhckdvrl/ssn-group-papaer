# L16 — Related Work and Novelty Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** paper identity locked in `README.md`  
**Verdict:** **Plausible independent contribution; bounded pilot authorized.** This is not a claim that no related paper exists anywhere. It means the strongest direct search found no work that already establishes the locked computation and consequence.

---

## 1. Locked paper identity

The central statement we are allowed to test is:

> **With the atomic hypotheses, evidence, target proposition, lexical inventory, and reasoning budget held fixed, arbitrary refinement/coarsening of the displayed hypothesis space may induce a directional prior over LLM credence toward the uniform mass on displayed cells (`1/M`). The key scientific question is whether this representational effect persists even when the model explicitly recognizes that the partitions carry no different information.**

The paper is **not** entitled to claim any of the following merely from a positive E01:

- LLM confidence is generally prompt-sensitive;
- LLM confidence is generally incoherent;
- adding/removing answer options changes confidence;
- retrieved-record grouping changes evidence weight;
- confidence affects abstention;
- humans show partition dependence;
- verbalized confidence is miscalibrated.

Those statements are already owned or substantially covered.

## 2. Classical parent: partition dependence is established

### Tversky & Koehler (1994) — Support Theory

**Reference:** Amos Tversky & Derek J. Koehler. *Support Theory: A Nonextensional Representation of Subjective Probability.* Psychological Review 101(4):547–567. DOI: `10.1037/0033-295X.101.4.547`.

**Owns:** the broad idea that probability judgment can depend on descriptions of extensionally identical events; unpacking focal or alternative hypotheses changes judged probability.

**Does not own:** LLM behavior, a modern language-model confidence computation, or the competence-versus-use decomposition proposed here.

### Fox & Rottenstreich (2003) — Partition Priming

**Reference:** Craig R. Fox & Yuval Rottenstreich. *Partition Priming in Judgment Under Uncertainty.* Psychological Science 14(3):195–200. DOI: `10.1111/1467-9280.02431`.

Canonical example: `Sunday hottest` can be mentally represented as a two-way case partition or a seven-way class partition, producing ignorance priors near `1/2` or `1/7`.

**Owns:** the classic behavioral phenomenon and the `1/M` ignorance-prior prediction in humans.

**Does not own:** whether an LLM's uncertainty is generated by the same representational dependency; whether a model can know two partitions are informationally equivalent yet still change its credence.

### Fox & Clemen (2005) — Bias toward the ignorance prior

**Reference:** Craig R. Fox & Robert T. Clemen. *Subjective Probability Assessment in Decision Analysis: Partition Dependence and Bias Toward the Ignorance Prior.* Management Science 51(9):1417–1432. DOI: `10.1287/mnsc.1050.0409`.

**Owns:** systematic pull toward a uniform distribution over the partition supplied to a human assessor; demonstrations across discrete/continuous state spaces and experts; knowledge attenuates the effect.

### See, Fox & Rottenstreich (2006) — learning and attenuation

**Reference:** Kelly E. See, Craig R. Fox, Yuval S. Rottenstreich. *Between Ignorance and Truth: Partition Dependence and Learning in Judgment Under Uncertainty.* JEP:LMC 32(6):1385–1402. DOI: `10.1037/0278-7393.32.6.1385`.

**Owns:** a compromise between learned frequencies/evidence and a partition-specific ignorance prior, and attenuation with stronger learned knowledge.

This supplies a preregistered mechanism prediction for L16: if the LLM effect is genuinely partition-prior-like rather than generic wording sensitivity, stronger evidence should attenuate it.

### Ding & Feldman (2025) — modern Registered Report replication

**Reference:** Y. Ding & G. Feldman. *Revisiting partition priming in judgement under uncertainty: replication and extension Registered Report of Fox and Rottenstreich (2003).* Royal Society Open Science (2025). DOI: `10.1098/rsos.250669`. Public project: `https://osf.io/g9czs/`.

N=603; mostly successful replication, with heterogeneity across items.

**Use for L16:** classic anchors / wording audit / comparison material. It is not our LLM novelty.

## 3. Closest modern LLM neighbors

### Matta, Naphade & Zou (2026) — coherent uncertainty and semantic invariance

**Reference:** *Rethinking Uncertainty Evaluation in Large Language Models.* arXiv:`2607.19367`.

This is the most dangerous conceptual neighbor.

**Owns:** a framework in which LLM confidence should satisfy structural coherence, faithfulness, and usefulness; explicit tests of **prompt semantic invariance** using ParaRel paraphrases; substantial invariance violations; CoT improves some metrics but leaves prompt semantic invariance essentially unchanged.

**Does not test:**

- refinement/coarsening of an explicitly represented hypothesis space;
- an exact fixed atomic alternative set under different partitions;
- a directional pull toward a partition-specific `1/M` prior;
- recognition of partition equivalence followed by a separate credence probe;
- partition-induced downstream decisions.

**Collision fence:** If L16 produces only `semantically equivalent prompts -> confidence differs`, Matta et al. already own the broad conclusion and L16 is **NO-GO**. L16 survives only if the partition manipulation supports a more specific representational law.

### Tian et al. (EMNLP 2023) — verbalized confidence

**Reference:** Katherine Tian et al. *Just Ask for Calibration: Strategies for Eliciting Calibrated Confidence Scores from Language Models Fine-Tuned with Human Feedback.* EMNLP 2023 Main, Anthology `2023.emnlp-main.330`, DOI `10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.330`.

**Owns:** methods for eliciting verbalized confidence and broad calibration results.

**Does not own:** hypothesis-space partition dependence. L16 uses verbalized confidence as one readout, not as novelty.

### Wang & Stengel-Eskin (ICLR 2026) — DINCO

**Reference:** *Calibrating Verbalized Confidence with Self-Generated Distractors.* ICLR 2026.

**Owns:** alternative claims/distractors as part of a training-free confidence-calibration method; normalized confidence across incompatible claims.

**Difference:** DINCO changes/generated the alternative claim set and uses it as an estimator. L16 keeps the atomic alternatives fixed and manipulates only their grouping. The question is whether **state-space representation itself creates belief**, not how to improve calibration with distractors.

### Wang et al. (2026) — multiple correct answers

**Reference:** *Evaluating and Calibrating LLM Confidence on Questions with Multiple Correct Answers.* arXiv:`2602.07842`.

**Owns:** answer cardinality changes and confidence under multiple valid answers.

**Difference:** semantic answer cardinality changes. L16's extension and atomic possibilities do not.

### Dai (2026) — response-scale design

**Reference:** *Rescaling Confidence: What Scale Design Reveals About LLM Metacognition.* arXiv:`2603.09309`.

**Owns:** the numerical confidence reporting scale itself changes verbalized metacognitive measurements.

**Difference:** L16 keeps the response scale fixed and changes the representation of the underlying hypothesis space. If L16 lives only in numeric verbalization and not another readout, this neighbor becomes fatal to a broad belief claim.

### Kumaran et al. (Nature Machine Intelligence 2026) — confidence causally drives behavior

**Reference:** Dharshan Kumaran et al. *Causal evidence that language models use confidence to drive behaviour.* Nature Machine Intelligence, published 2026-09-07. DOI: `10.1038/s42256-026-01293-x`.

**Owns:** internal/verbal confidence predicts and causally governs abstention; steering confidence changes behavior.

**Use for L16:** motivates the consequence of partition-induced confidence, but it means L16 cannot claim merely that confidence affects decisions. A downstream C3 would ask whether an informationally irrelevant partition changes a confidence-mediated decision.

### Liu, Zhou & Wang (2026) — Record Grouping Controls Evidence Weight

**Reference:** Zhongxuan Liu, Sicheng Zhou, Hongzhi Wang. *Record Grouping Controls Evidence Weight in Language Models.* arXiv:`2609.08698`, submitted 2026-09-08.

This is a **fresh and dangerous neighbor**.

**Owns:** supplied grouping/partition of retrieved input records changes how evidence is weighted; false splits/merges can produce large decision shifts.

**Difference:** its manipulated object is the **partition of evidence records** before generation. L16 manipulates the **partition of mutually exclusive hypotheses/outcomes**, while holding evidence and all atoms fixed.

**Collision fence:** L16 cannot conclude only that "grouping matters" or "representation affects decisions." Those broad conclusions are already owned. The surviving object must be the state-space/credence law: equivalent hypothesis partitions induce a `1/M`-directed prior despite unchanged evidence.

## 4. Strongest honest compression

> **Support Theory + Fox/Rottenstreich/Fox/Clemen** already establish human non-extensional probability judgment and partition-specific ignorance priors.  
> **Matta et al. 2026** already establish that LLM confidence can violate semantic invariance.  
> **Tian/DINCO/MACE/Rescaling Confidence** already establish that confidence readout is sensitive to elicitation and alternative-set design.  
> **Liu et al. 2026** already establish that supplied grouping of evidence records can change LLM evidence weight.  
> **Kumaran et al. 2026** already establish that LLM confidence can causally affect abstention.

### What remains if all of those are granted?

No direct owner was found for this statement:

> **When the world states and evidence are literally unchanged, arbitrary refinement/coarsening of the same explicitly enumerated hypothesis space induces a quantitative, directional `1/M` prior in LLM credence; this can occur even when the model correctly reports that the partitions are informationally equivalent.**

That is more specific than generic prompt sensitivity and different from evidence-record grouping or alternative-set cardinality.

If the pilot does not support this specific statement, the current paper identity dies.

## 5. Prospective development-path novelty

This audit is intentionally prospective so the project cannot repeat L12/L13-style drift.

### If E01 finds a partition effect

The next claim is **not** automatically "LLMs are non-extensional." Matta already makes broad coherence/invariance claims. E02 must determine whether the effect follows the preregistered partition-prior law rather than generic prompt dependence.

### If E02 shows `1/M`-directed movement + equivalence awareness

Surviving paper identity:

> **Extensional knowledge does not guarantee extensional uncertainty: LLMs can recognize that two representations encode the same hypothesis space while constructing different credences from the displayed partition.**

This remains distinct from current neighbors.

### If only verbal confidence shifts

The paper mutates toward **confidence elicitation / response-format sensitivity** and must return to selection. Do not rescue it automatically.

### If a nonverbal/readout-invariant effect survives

Only then may C3 be considered after re-selection: does the same arbitrary state-space partition cross an abstention/decision threshold? Kumaran et al. own confidence→behavior generally; our new inference would be **representation-induced uncertainty → behavior under an informationally equivalent hypothesis space**.

### If the effect instead becomes evidence grouping / option omission / distractor count / order bias

Immediate mutation reset. Liu et al., MACE/DINCO, or generic MCQ literature may own those stories.

# Verdict

## **PASS FOR BOUNDED PILOT**

The area is adjacent to a newly crowded uncertainty/coherence literature, so the claim is deliberately narrow in *scientific object* but broad in consequence. **Generic confidence sensitivity is not enough.** Only the preregistered hypothesis-partition effect can carry authorization.
