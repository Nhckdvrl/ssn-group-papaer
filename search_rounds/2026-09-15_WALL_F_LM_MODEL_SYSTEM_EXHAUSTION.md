# 1. Mother scientific problem

The durable question is older and broader than LLMs:

> **When may evidence obtained from an experimentally accessible surrogate system support a scientific claim about a different target system?**

For language science the surrogate is a neural language model and the target is some aspect of human language learning, processing, representation, or language-level typology.

The key correction is that `LM resembles humans` is not one scientific claim. At least four different links are commonly conflated:

1. **behavioral link** — model and human make similar judgments / errors;
2. **computational link** — model and human solve the same abstract problem or exhibit the same inductive preference;
3. **mechanistic link** — the relevant causal organization is shared at some justified level of abstraction;
4. **extrapolative link** — an effect discovered by manipulating the model should also hold in humans / human language.

A paper must specify which link it needs and why that link is warranted.

# 2. Deep ancestry: linking a surrogate to a target is already a mature scientific problem

## 2.1 Teller 1984 — linking propositions

Teller's classic `Linking propositions` treats the connection between perceptual states and physiological states as an explicit scientific assumption rather than something supplied automatically by correlation. The paper distinguishes families such as identity, similarity, mutual exclusivity, simplicity, and analogy, and explicitly asks about testability and falsifiability.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6395480/

This matters because a modern statement such as

> `LM variable X tracks human phenomenon Y`

is not self-interpreting. The scientific content depends on the proposition that licenses moving from X to Y.

## 2.2 Cognitive model discrimination — ordinary fit is insufficient

Myung & Pitt (Psychological Review 2009) formalize optimal experimental design for discriminating competing cognitive models. If rival models agree on ordinary stimuli, one should seek experimental conditions where their predictions diverge. The scientific object is not average fit but **differential prediction under an informative design**.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2743521/

Golan, Raju & Kriegeskorte (PNAS 2020) turn this into `controversial stimuli` for neural networks as models of human vision: synthesize stimuli on which competing networks disagree, then let human responses adjudicate among them.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7703564/

Most damaging for novelty, Golan et al. (Nature Machine Intelligence 2023) already transfer this exact strategy to **language models and human language judgments**, generating sentence pairs on which LMs disagree and using human judgments to identify the more human-consistent model.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-023-00718-1

Therefore the generic proposal

> `do not compare average human–LM correlation; find diagnostic language stimuli where models disagree`

is directly owned.

## 2.3 Mechanistic similarity — also a mature linking problem

Cao & Yamins (Cognitive Systems Research 2024) explicitly ask when deep neural networks that mimic animal behavior can count as explanatory / mechanistic models of the brain. Their 3M++ proposal requires a justified mapping of model and target to a common runnable level of abstraction rather than behavioral resemblance alone.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S138904172400038X

This is continuous with older `model-to-mechanism mapping` and with the broader model-based neuroscience literature. Neural measurements can break some behavioral model mimicry, but additional observables do not automatically identify a mechanism: distinct mechanisms can still produce similar neural dynamics.

Representative source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28392584/

Therefore generic descendants such as

> `match human and LM internals`,  
> `use mechanistic interpretability to validate the LM as a cognitive model`,

are not new scientific parents.

## 2.4 Cross-system extrapolation — the model-organism literature already owns the general logic

Philosophy of experiment and model-organism science has long studied exactly the inference:

> effect/mechanism in accessible model system → effect/mechanism in less-accessible target system.

Steel's **comparative process tracing** asks researchers to identify the causal mechanism in the model, locate points where target and model may differ, and test whether those differences block extrapolation.

Source trail:
- https://academic.oup.com/book/5931/chapter-abstract/149254609
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10539-025-09981-5

A 2026 review of model-organism validity explicitly treats comparative process tracing, mechanistic evidence, construct validity, predictive history, and interfering effects as distinct warrants for model→human extrapolation.

Source: https://faseb.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1096/fj.202600104R

Thus the attractive criterion

> **apply the same external perturbation to LM and humans and trust the LM only if the response function matches**

is scientifically sensible, but not a new parent. It is a modern computational-language instantiation of a mature extrapolation principle.

# 3. The 2025–2026 language-model debate already directly owns the WALL

## 3.1 Futrell & Mahowald — LMs as model systems / proof-of-concept systems

Their BBS target article argues that LMs can contribute to language science and can serve as model systems / working proofs of concept for gradient, usage-based accounts. They explicitly distinguish this from the claim that LMs replace linguistic theory.

Source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/how-linguistics-learned-to-stop-worrying-and-love-the-language-models/2395C24EB472B60B2514F7D5F93EB9A8

Their 2026 response to 25 commentaries continues the dispute rather than settling it.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42379988/

## 3.2 Resnik — the Marr-level objection

Resnik's 2026 BBS commentary argues that the `LM as model system` claim fails or is poorly motivated at different Marr levels: not implementation-level models, weakly justified algorithmic/representational models, and problematic computational-level models. LMs may be scientific tools without thereby being cognitive models.

Source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/are-language-models-models/DB84BF5BEB94FCEBB180163AA3478A65

## 3.3 Kallini & Potts — an explicit phased linking-hypothesis program

Kallini & Potts (BBS 2026) explicitly propose refining LM architectures so that they better discriminate possible from impossible languages and gradually building **linking hypotheses to human cognition**.

Source: https://juliekallini.com/papers/

Therefore the headline

> `What linking hypothesis would justify using LMs as evidence about human language?`

is not an unclaimed question. It is the center of an active explicit debate.

# 4. Failed inferences already visible inside language science

The wall is important because several apparently natural bridges have already failed.

## 4.1 Better next-word prediction ≠ better model of human processing

Oh & Schuler (TACL 2023) find that larger, lower-perplexity Transformer LMs provide **poorer** surprisal-based fits to human reading times. Follow-up work shows the inverse scaling is not explained away by simple data leakage.

Sources:
- https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00548/115371/
- https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.91/

This breaks a tempting linking assumption:

> better statistical model of text → better cognitive model of human prediction.

But this failure is already a direct research program. 2026 work argues that LMs can become `superhuman` predictors because of data and memory advantages, while other work shows that internal layers can sometimes recover stronger human alignment.

Sources:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13352589/
- https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.78/

A generic `find the right layer / context limit / humanized memory` project is therefore occupied.

## 4.2 Adding a human-like constraint does not guarantee a more human-like behavioral model

Thamma & Heilbron (TACL 2026) train Transformers with fleeting memory. The constraint improves language learning and targeted syntax but **worsens** surprisal-based reading-time prediction, and the authors report that prior accounts of the LM-performance / human-fit dissociation do not explain the result.

Source: https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item3715688/human-fleeting-memory-improves-language-learning-impairs-reading-time

This is a valuable warning for IP07:

> local biological plausibility is not monotonic evidence of global model validity.

But turning that warning into `test another human-like constraint` would be old model-validity logic × LM and also risks resurrecting the just-closed resource-constraint WALL-D.

## 4.3 Individual learnability ≠ population typology

Rafferty, Griffiths & Ettlinger (Cognition 2013) show that greater learnability of a feature is **not sufficient** for that feature to become a cultural universal under transmission. Spontaneous production and the structure / multiplicity of alternatives can reverse the population outcome.

This breaks another common bridge:

> an LM learns linguistic pattern A more easily → A should be typologically preferred / human-natural.

The classic iterated-learning and cultural-evolution program already owns the missing transmission dynamics.

Recent possible/impossible-language work has rediscovered the issue. Janarthan, Haley & Goldwater (CoNLL 2026 Best) argue that perplexity/sample efficiency are weak bridges to non-attestation and compare grammatical sensitivity with generative-production failure, with the latter showing much stronger degradation at long lengths.

This is scientifically strong but does not make `run LM iterated learning` an unowned parent.

## 4.4 Human-like output ≠ human-like cause

Cognitive model mimicry is decades old: different mechanisms can fit the same behavior. Additional neural/internal measures can help but also remain non-identifying when different mechanisms produce similar dynamics.

Hence

> `LM and humans show the same bias`

cannot by itself identify

> `LM reveals why humans show the bias`.

This is not an LLM-specific discovery.

# 5. Exhausted candidate-shaped descendants

The following routes were inspected and should be treated as anti-resurrection state.

## F1 — Human–LM response-function alignment under matched perturbations

Temptation:

> compare `Δhuman` and `ΔLM` under the same evidence / memory / stimulus manipulation; a matching causal fingerprint should be stronger evidence than static correlation.

Why it dies as a standalone parent:

- model discrimination / diagnostic experimental design already says to test where theories diverge;
- comparative process tracing / model-organism extrapolation already uses matched causal structure / relevant differences to warrant transfer;
- language-specific instantiation without a substantive old dispute becomes `external-validity principle × LM`.

**Verdict: KILL GENERIC FORM.**

## F2 — Controversial language stimuli as the criterion for cognitive validity

Directly owned by Golan et al. 2023.

**Verdict: DIRECT OWNER.**

## F3 — Internal-layer / mechanism matching as stronger linking evidence

Directly adjacent to 3M++, model-based neuroscience, causal-abstraction work, and 2025 TACL human-like-internals work.

If the project only says `behavior can differ from internal alignment`, it is already owned. If it asks for identical human/LM mechanisms, the mapping itself becomes the unsolved theory.

**Verdict: CROWDED / IDENTIFICATION FAILURE.**

## F4 — Build a scalar `model-system validity score`

This would collapse construct validity, behavioral prediction, mechanism, training history, and extrapolative success into an arbitrary metric. The model-organism literature explicitly treats these as distinct evidence types.

**Verdict: NO NATURAL SCIENTIFIC QUANTITY.**

## F5 — Humanize the LM until human fit improves

Data scale, memory, objective, architecture, interaction, multimodality, etc. can all be manipulated. But selecting whichever modification raises human correlation is method-first model fitting and does not answer a prior scientific question. Direct programs already study human-scale data and memory limitations.

**Verdict: MODEL-FITTING PROGRAM, NOT A NEW QUESTION.**

## F6 — Possible/impossible language as the linking test

This is the current Kallini / Potts / Futrell / Mahowald / Resnik / Bowers / Lan / Xu / Janarthan debate and was already deeply mapped under WALL-B. Reopening it here would merely rename WALL-B.

**Verdict: ANTI-RESURRECTION — RETURN TO WALL-B STATE.**

## F7 — Surprisal / reading-time inverse scaling as the residual anomaly

The anomaly is real and stable enough to matter, but it already has direct owners proposing memory/data/superhuman prediction explanations, internal-layer reinterpretations, context-limitation interventions, and 2026 counterevidence in which fleeting memory improves learning while worsening reading-time prediction.

A fresh paper would need an independently motivated cognitive theory that predicts a specific unresolved crossover; `which tweak restores human fit?` is not enough.

**Verdict: ACTIVE OWNER DENSITY; NO CURRENT ENTRY.**

# 6. Why there is no Main-level residual quantity

After lineage saturation, the generic question decomposes rather than crystallizes.

For any proposed LM→human inference, one must first specify a **substantive scientific object**:

- a particular learning bias;
- a particular processing mechanism;
- a particular causal constraint;
- a particular typological transmission process;
- a particular abstract computation.

Only then can a linking hypothesis say what correspondence between LM and human would count as evidence.

This means IP07 is primarily a **meta-level validity requirement**. It cannot supply the substantive object by itself.

The tempting residual

> `What is the universally correct linking criterion?`

is not well posed. Different scientific claims require different warrants. Behavioral prediction, mechanistic explanation, computational proof of concept, and cross-system causal extrapolation are not the same estimand.

The alternative

> `Which linking criterion best predicts whether an LM finding replicates in humans?`

would require a benchmark / retrospective corpus of LM→human claims and becomes exactly the evaluator/benchmark direction the project does not want.

Therefore there is currently **no single same-object / same-unit / same-observable / same-treatment disagreement** from which a new standalone L-series naturally follows.

# 8. Exhaustion verdict

**WALL-F / IP07 is EXHAUSTED AS A STANDALONE TOPIC GENERATOR FOR THE CURRENT SEARCH.**

No L-series is registered. No pilot is authorized.

This is a healthy zero-survivor result because the wall was not killed by a single owner paper. The full ancestry was reconstructed across:

- psychophysical linking propositions;
- cognitive model discrimination;
- controversial-stimulus severe tests;
- neural mechanistic mapping;
- model-organism extrapolation;
- human psycholinguistic modeling;
- possible/impossible-language / inductive-bias debates;
- cultural transmission.

The literature does not leave a natural unowned scalar or intervention that answers the generic linking question.

# 9. What IP07 should become in the search process

Retain IP07 as a **mandatory evidence-standard audit** for future substantive candidates.

Whenever a project uses an LM to say something about humans / human language, force the following questions:

1. What exactly is the target claim: behavioral, computational, mechanistic, or extrapolative?
2. What is the explicit linking proposition?
3. What target-system evidence would falsify that link?
4. Are model and target matched on the causal feature the claim actually depends on, rather than merely on outputs?
5. Is the result diagnostic among rival theories, or can multiple mechanisms mimic it?
6. If an intervention is performed only on the LM, what warrants transporting the effect to humans?
7. Does the link survive a held-out / severe test not used to choose the model or representation?

A project that cannot answer these questions should not make a human-science inference from model behavior.

But these questions are **selection constraints**, not themselves our next paper.

---

# 10. Reopen condition

Reopen WALL-F as a topic generator only if a future substantive language-science dispute supplies all of:

1. two mature accounts about the **same target phenomenon**;
2. both currently fit the available human evidence;
3. a model system creates a genuinely new identifying operation unavailable in the target;
4. an independently justified linking proposition says why that operation transports to the target;
5. the rival accounts predict opposite outcomes under the same operation;
6. positive, opposite, and null outcomes remain scientifically interpretable;
7. reviewer compression does not reduce the result to generic model validation / human–LM similarity.

Until then:

> **Use IP07 to police inference. Do not use it to generate topics.**
