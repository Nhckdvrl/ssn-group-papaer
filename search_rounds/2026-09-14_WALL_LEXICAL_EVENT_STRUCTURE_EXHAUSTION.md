# 1. Mother scientific problem

A long tradition in lexical semantics asks whether apparently complex verb meanings are implemented through reusable semantic structure.

Canonical examples include change-of-state and lexical causative verbs, with analyses of the rough form:

`CAUSE(BECOME(ROOT/STATE))`

The strong scientific claim is not merely that such a formula can describe the verb's entailments. It is that the learner's semantic system actually reuses some structured event computation across lexical items and constructions.

This is old enough to predate neural networks. Fodor's classic attack on deriving *kill* from *cause to die* already separated a useful logical paraphrase from a psychologically/computationally real lexical decomposition. Later psycholinguistic work tried to use priming and processing cost to argue for internal event complexity.

The durable question is therefore:

> **When a lexical item supports systematically decomposable entailments, are the decomposed parts actually reusable units of the computation, or only a successful analyst-level description?**

This is a legitimate WALL. The failure below is about identification and ownership, not importance.

# 2. Historical lineage and why the naive H1 binary is wrong

## 2.1 Classic template/root decomposition

A major event-structural tradition decomposes verb meaning into:

- a small reusable **event template** / structural component (ACT, CAUSE, BECOME, etc.);
- a more idiosyncratic **root** carrying verb-specific content.

In strong versions, the template bears much of the burden for regular grammatical behaviour such as argument realization and alternations.

Psycholinguistic studies have reported processing costs associated with greater event-structure complexity, motivating claims that event templates have psychological reality. However, processing-time evidence does not by itself establish that unrelated lexical items reuse the same internal semantic variable.

## 2.2 Root-enriched theories remove the clean `template vs root` opposition

Beavers & Koontz-Garboden's root-semantics program challenges strong template/root bifurcation. Roots can themselves contribute change, causation, result, manner, and other information that older accounts often allocated to templates.

Beavers & Jerro (Natural Language & Linguistic Theory, 2026), **The role of root semantics in determining argument alternations**, continues this update: root-specific meaning is central to alternation behaviour, and root meaning can overlap with information that is also expressed structurally/templatically. The resulting theory is explicitly multifactorial rather than `template or root`.

Therefore the original H1 wording — reusable template versus holistic lexeme — creates a false binary. Modern lexical semantics allows structured event representations **and** semantically rich roots **and** construction/syntactic contributions.

## 2.3 Construction/exoskeletal accounts add a third locus

Argument-structure and Construction Grammar traditions place more of the event/argument structure in syntactic or constructional configurations. This creates a genuine locus-of-composition question, but it also means that the scientific state is not reducible to two mutually exclusive accounts.

The correct historical state is closer to:

> root semantics + lexical/event structure + syntactic/constructional composition may all contribute, and the live question is their division of labour.

That state is scientifically healthy but makes a generic `which representation does the LM use?` project too unconstrained.

# 3. Modern theory update: even decompositional accounts disagree on *what the decomposition is*

McNally (Glossa, 2024), **Two approaches to semantic decomposition of change-of-state verbs...**, distinguishes at least two substantive decomposition traditions rather than treating decomposition as a single hypothesis:

- primitive-predicate / lexical-decomposition analyses built from operators such as CAUSE/BECOME;
- event-part / mereotopological decompositions in which structured event parts and states need not correspond to the same stock of discrete primitives.

More sharply, Martin, Schäfer & Piñón (Glossa, 2025), **Transitives with inchoative semantics**, challenges the standard assumption that transitive change-of-state causatives necessarily contain a separate causing event plus a change event.

Their proposed analysis gives the verb a **single verbal event plus a result state**. The relation between them is interpreted as BECOME/inchoative by default; an agentive external argument/Voice can induce a causal interpretation. Their evidence includes progressive interpretation, temporal modification, `start/begin`, and anaphoric diagnostics.

This supplies a much cleaner live theoretical dispute than the naive H1:

> **Does a lexical causative itself project a separate cause event, or can causation be introduced compositionally by Voice/external-argument structure over a one-event + result-state verb meaning?**

This is a real 2025 formal-semantics dispute.

# 4. Direct modern owners / crowded neighbours

The modern model regime does not enter an empty space.

## 4.1 Lexical semantics with LMs is already a direct research program

Petersen & Potts, Findings EACL 2023, **Lexical Semantics with Large Language Models: A Case Study of English `break`**, studies one of the canonical change-of-state / causative-alternation verbs. The paper argues that contextual LLM representations capture established sense distinctions and uses the comparison to question traditional commitments to discrete lexical-semantic features.

Important consequence for us:

> `causative alternation + LLM representation + implications for lexical-semantic ontology` is already a direct parent, not an open field.

A project that merely finds dense internal encodings of CAUSE/RESULT, or argues that semantic features are graded rather than discrete, is a successor.

## 4.2 Theory comparison in lexical semantics is not novel by itself

Branco et al., COLING 2020, **Comparative Probing of Lexical Semantics Theories for Cognitive Plausibility and Technological Usefulness**, explicitly compares families of lexical-semantic representation theories using computational probes and cognitive/technological criteria.

Thus `compare lexical-semantic theories using neural representations` is not a new parent.

## 4.3 Argument-structure abstraction / learning is already well occupied

- Thrush, Wilcox & Levy, BlackboxNLP 2020, **Investigating Novel Verb Learning in BERT**, trains novel verbs in one alternation frame and measures transfer to the sister frame.
- ACL 2022 **The Neural Reality of Argument Structure Constructions** uses psycholinguistically inspired tests to study construction-level organization.
- TACL 2023 **How Abstract Is Linguistic Generalization in LLMs? Experiments with Argument Structure** tests abstract generalization and higher-order transfer.
- 2026 Construction Grammar work studies construction meaning, coercion, productivity, and statistical preemption.

Therefore `learning transfer reveals shared causative structure` is also an occupied route.

## 4.4 Linguistically motivated mechanistic analysis is already an active program

Relevant nearby owners include:

- EMNLP 2025 Outstanding: **Causal Interventions Reveal Shared Structure Across English Filler–Gap Constructions** — distributed interchange interventions test whether superficially different constructions reuse a causal variable.
- Findings ACL 2026: **Emergence and Localisation of Semantic Role Circuits in Large Language Models** — causal circuits for predicate/argument semantic roles.
- ACL 2026 short work such as **UERLens** studies internal causal/temporal/subevent relations and intervention.
- LREC 2026: **Mechanistic Interpretability Meets Cognitive Linguistics: Modelling Locative Image Schemas in the Circuit Framework** directly exemplifies `classic cognitive-linguistic theory -> mechanistic circuit analysis`.

Hence `old semantic theory + mechanistic interpretability` is no longer an independent contribution shape.

# 5. The attractive operation that failed identification

A seemingly strong idea was a **cross-form causal reuse test**:

1. identify a RESULT/BECOME-like causal variable only from explicit compositional expressions such as `die`, `become dead`, or explicit `cause X to die`;
2. freeze the mapping;
3. test whether that variable transfers zero-shot into lexical causatives such as `kill`, `shatter`, `flatten`, etc.;
4. use held-out verbs/constructions so the lexical causative itself never trains the alignment.

This initially looked capable of distinguishing:

- **decomposition**: `kill` internally reuses a result/change subcomputation;
- **lexical atomism / meaning-postulate**: `kill` is lexical-atomic and only entails `die/dead` downstream.

It does **not** identify that distinction.

## Fatal confound: downstream semantic convergence

An atomist model can compute:

`atomic KILL meaning -> ordinary semantic inference -> victim becomes dead`

An explicitly decompositional expression can compute:

`CAUSE/BECOME/DEAD -> victim becomes dead`

The two routes may converge on the **same downstream event/situation representation**. A successful cross-form patch would then prove reusable downstream semantic content, not that `KILL`'s lexical representation was itself decomposed.

This is not fixable by simply moving the probe/patch to an earlier layer. A neural model can invoke the lexical entailment immediately after lexical access, and transformer layer number has no theory-independent mapping onto `lexical representation` versus `post-lexical inference`.

Therefore the strongest apparent identification for classic decomposition vs atomism is invalid.

# 6. The narrower live theory: one event vs two events

The Martin–Schäfer–Piñón 2025 dispute avoids some of the atomism confound because it concerns an explicit structural commitment:

- **traditional two-event account:** lexical causative contains a causing event and a change/result event;
- **one-event + result-state account:** the verbal meaning contains one event plus a result state; causation can be introduced by agentive Voice/external-argument composition.

This suggests a candidate-shaped question:

> **For a change-of-state verb, does the learned computation contain a lexical cause event, or does causation enter compositionally through the external argument/Voice?**

The same lexical verb under agentive vs non-agentive causers is an attractive natural contrast.

However, the candidate does not survive the identification/outcome audit.

---

# 7. Why causal-abstraction theory comparison does not rescue it

One could specify the one-event and two-event theories as two high-level causal models and ask which gives higher interchange-intervention faithfulness on held-out verbs and held-out semantic diagnostics.

To make this remotely defensible, the experiment would need at minimum:

- the same low-complexity (ideally linear/restricted) alignment class for both theories;
- equal search budgets;
- alignments learned on one set of verbs/diagnostics and evaluated on untouched verbs plus a different diagnostic family;
- no theory-specific tuning;
- counterfactual predictions fixed before low-level search;
- active controls for lexical identity, syntax, agentivity, and ordinary entailment competence.

These constraints are necessary because causal-abstraction alignment is not self-validating. Recent work shows that flexible alignment choices can make interpretation too permissive, and 2025 work on combining causal models shows that different high-level models can partially explain different network states.

But even with the strictest version, the **result space is unhealthy**.

### Outcome A — one-event model uniquely wins

Scientifically interesting. It would suggest that the neural learner implements something closer to one-event + result-state semantics with causation compositionally introduced.

### Outcome B — two-event model uniquely wins

Also scientifically interesting. It would support a genuinely bieventive learned causative computation.

### Outcome C — both theories fit well

This is not a clean lexical-semantic result. A rich neural computation may admit multiple valid high-level abstractions at different resolutions or in different input states. The project collapses toward the generic interpretability question of explanation non-uniqueness / model combination.

### Outcome D — neither theory fits

This is non-identifying. It could mean:

- the model implements a richer root-sensitive / construction-sensitive mixture;
- the high-level theory variables are wrong for this learner;
- the alignment class is too restrictive;
- the intervention creates off-manifold states;
- the search simply failed to find the relevant representation.

The null cannot distinguish scientific falsification from instrument failure.

### Consequence

The paper becomes Main-level only in the clean A/B-winner outcomes. The other major outcomes do not answer the mother question decisively.

That is **mechanistic phenomenon gambling**, not an outcome-robust research question.

# 9. Branches explicitly closed in this WALL

Do not resurrect these by changing model, verb list, prompt, or interpretability tool:

1. **`event template vs holistic verb`** — false binary after root-enriched / constructional theories.
2. **`template vs root`** — modern theory is multifactorial; roots can contribute templatic information.
3. **`lexical decomposition vs atomism via cross-form patching`** — downstream semantic convergence does not identify lexical decomposition.
4. **`early vs late layer proves lexical vs post-lexical`** — no theory-independent layer/stage mapping.
5. **`causative/inchoative shared representation`** — already mature in psycholinguistics, argument-structure learning, CxG, and LM representation work.
6. **`novel-verb transfer proves shared event template`** — novel-verb alternation transfer is an established human and neural-learning paradigm.
7. **`root meaning + construction meaning integration`** — old Construction Grammar object with active 2026 coercion/preemption/productivity programs.
8. **`find CAUSE/BECOME/RESULT circuit`** — causal semantic-feature/circuit parent is active; localization is not the scientific question.
9. **`one-event vs two-event by LM judgments`** — Martin et al. already supply linguistic diagnostics; repeating them on LMs is old-theory x LM.
10. **`one-event vs two-event by unrestricted causal abstraction`** — alignment/non-uniqueness and adverse-outcome ambiguity prevent decisive inference.

# 10. Reopen condition

Reopen this WALL only if at least one of the following genuinely new forms of leverage appears:

1. an **independently justified intervention on lexical-semantic structure** whose semantics are fixed without learning a representation map from the same target behaviour;
2. a natural learning situation in which rival event-structure theories make **opposite transfer predictions on the same quantity**, beyond already-studied verb-alternation / construction generalization;
3. a formal result showing that one rival high-level event model and the other are **not simultaneously realizable abstractions** under a sufficiently restricted, independently justified alignment class;
4. a new empirical law in natural language whose explanation genuinely requires distinguishing event-variable cardinality / locus of causation and cannot be predicted from existing linguistic diagnostics.

A new model family, more verbs, SAE features, a better probe, a different patching method, or narrower wording is **not** a reopen condition.

# 12. Final state

**No L number. No pilot. No candidate promotion.**

The WALL produced a useful negative scientific state:

> lexical/event semantics remains an important unresolved domain, and 2024–2026 theory is actively revising the ontology and locus of causation. However, the obvious neural-model leverage either repeats an active lexical/argument-structure program, fails to distinguish lexical structure from downstream semantic inference, or depends on a causal-abstraction result whose non-A/B outcomes are not scientifically decisive.

This is exactly where the search should stop rather than preserve novelty by narrowing the claim.
