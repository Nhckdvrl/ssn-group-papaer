## Pool A — Native stochastic generation

### A1 — Knowing a distribution ≠ implementing it as a generator
**Question.** When a model can state the correct target distribution, what computation fails between that knowledge and its emitted samples?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Probability sampling is a basic requirement for stochastic agents; recent LLM work reports a large gap between articulated probabilities and realized samples.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Correct verbal probability and empirical sample frequencies are not the same quantity; failure could arise from positional priors, deterministic decoding policy, autoregressive self-conditioning, or an absent sampling computation.  
**Ideal observable.** A decomposition of target-distribution representation, token-level action logits, and realized independent-sample frequencies under matched semantics.  
**Best-case consequence.** Identify whether LLM stochasticity is a readout/compilation problem or whether no reusable sampling computation exists.  
**Abstract headline if beautiful.** `Language models can represent a target distribution without compiling it into a matching action distribution.`  
**Taste.** Strong pressure, but high owner risk.

### A2 — Batch randomness vs independent-call randomness
**Question.** Why can within-response batches look more distribution-like than stateless independent calls if both ostensibly sample from the same model?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Modern LLM APIs expose token sampling, yet recent audits report strong protocol asymmetry.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** The two protocols differ in autoregressive context, not target distribution; context may be acting as a compensatory counting controller rather than a sampler.  
**Ideal observable.** Conditional next-sample corrections as a function of prior within-batch outcomes versus stateless calls.  
**Best-case consequence.** Reinterpret apparent random-generation competence as deterministic empirical-frequency balancing.  
**Headline.** `What looks like sampling is sequence-level self-correction.`  
**Taste.** Interesting but already too close to the native-sampling parent.

### A3 — Exchangeability as the missing property
**Question.** Are LLM-generated draws non-exchangeable even when the requested probabilistic process is exchangeable?  
**Scientific ancestry.** IID sampling implies order-invariant joint distributions; autoregressive generation creates ordered conditional dependence by construction.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** A marginal histogram can look right while the joint process is wrong.  
**Ideal observable.** Permutation-sensitive joint tests, lag dependence, and conditional distribution drift with identical marginals.  
**Best-case consequence.** Replace `randomness quality` with a precise computational failure: inability to realize exchangeable stochastic processes natively.  
**Headline.** `Correct marginals conceal a non-exchangeable generator.`  
**Taste.** Better quantity than generic randomness, but still downstream of an occupied parent.

### A4 — Probability semantics vs probability control
**Question.** Are numerical/verbal probabilities merely semantic content, or can they serve as control variables over the model’s own generation frequency?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Calibration and probability understanding are widely studied, but a probability used descriptively need not be a control knob for self-generation.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** The same probability value can be interpreted correctly without inducing matching behavior.  
**Ideal observable.** Monotonicity and compositionality of action frequency under probability interventions, with semantics fixed.  
**Best-case consequence.** Separate probability representation from endogenous stochastic control.  
**Headline.** `Probability meaning and probability control are distinct computations.`  
**Taste.** Reviewer compression to A1 / cognition–behavior gap.

## Pool B — Factual memory and recall architecture

### B1 — Is factual recall mechanism architecture-conditional?
**Question.** What architectural condition determines whether factual recall is attention-dominated, MLP-dominated, or geometrically distributed?  
**Scientific ancestry.** GPT-style work motivated the MLP-as-key-value-memory story, but newer cross-architecture work reports Qwen-like attention-heavy recall and newer theory proposes geometric memorization.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** These are potentially different mechanisms for the same function, not merely different saliency maps.  
**Ideal observable.** A predictive architecture-level variable that forecasts recall mechanism before probing the model.  
**Best-case consequence.** Turn model-specific exceptions into a conditional law of factual memory.  
**Headline.** `Factual recall mechanism is controlled by architecture, not universally stored in MLP key–value memories.`  
**Taste.** Attractive, but likely a direct successor program.

### B2 — Associative memory vs geometric selection
**Question.** Do full-scale pretrained LMs store facts as pairwise associations, or as relational geometry plus reusable selectors?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Classical mechanistic work often describes MLPs as associative memories; 2026 theory shows a qualitatively different geometric solution can be learned.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Both mechanisms can answer the same factual queries but predict different transfer, capacity and editing behavior.  
**Ideal observable.** Relation-selector transfer to held-out entity mappings and edits that preserve selector function while changing geometry.  
**Best-case consequence.** Replace a dominant mental model of factual storage.  
**Headline.** `Transformers retrieve relations geometrically rather than by storing one key–value association per fact.`  
**Taste.** Scientifically strong, but owner density is already very high.

### B3 — One-to-many knowledge: recall vs suppression
**Question.** Is enumerating a fact set a single shared recall computation plus history-dependent suppression, or repeated retrieval conditioned on already emitted answers?  
**Scientific ancestry.** One-to-many recall requires both remembering valid answers and avoiding repetition.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Identical final lists can arise from different internal decompositions.  
**Ideal observable.** Causal contribution of subject, previous answers, attention and MLP updates across list positions.  
**Best-case consequence.** Establish a general law for autoregressive set recall.  
**Headline.** `Autoregressive enumeration separates global recall from local suppression.`  
**Taste.** Directly owned by Promote–Suppress–Iterate.

### B4 — Can knowledge mechanism be predicted from representation geometry?
**Question.** Does the geometry of entity/relation embeddings predict whether the model will rely on attention, MLP selection, or memorized association at recall time?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Cross-architecture recall and geometric-memory theory jointly suggest representational organization may be upstream of apparent module specialization.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** This would be a hidden-condition law rather than another localization result.  
**Ideal observable.** Pre-registered geometric statistic predicting intervention-defined recall routes across architectures.  
**Best-case consequence.** Explain divergent mechanistic findings with a common upstream quantity.  
**Headline.** `Representation geometry predicts where factual recall computation lives.`  
**Taste.** Tempting, but currently A+B and successor-shaped; no independent pressure yet.

## Pool C — Translation laws under pretrained generative models

### C1 — Is translationese a translation property or an SFT-acquired policy?
**Question.** Does translationese arise from translation itself or from supervised translation policy imposed on a pretrained natural-language model?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Human translation studies treat translationese as a broad regularity; pretrained LMs begin from large natural-text distributions.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Pretraining and SFT separate target-language competence from translation behavior in a way classical MT did not.  
**Ideal observable.** Same base model before/after translation SFT, matched source semantics, with natural-target distribution as reference.  
**Best-case consequence.** Reclassify an apparent translation universal as a training-induced policy.  
**Headline.** `Translationese is learned during supervised translation rather than inherited from target-language modeling.`  
**Taste.** Directly owned in ACL 2025.

### C2 — Simplification as universal vs regime-conditional tendency
**Question.** Does syntactic simplification remain a translation law when the translator is a pretrained generative LM rather than a human or task-specific MT system?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Simplification is a long-standing translation-universal hypothesis.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Pretraining supplies fluent target-language priors and different capacity constraints.  
**Ideal observable.** Same source content across human, LLM and original target text with syntactic complexity trajectories.  
**Best-case consequence.** Turn a putative universal into a translator-regime conditional law.  
**Headline.** `Translation simplification is not universal; its magnitude depends on the translator’s computational regime.`  
**Taste.** Already directly tested in 2025.

### C3 — Explicitation: semantic necessity vs translation policy
**Question.** When LLM translations make implicit relations explicit, is this driven by source ambiguity resolution or by an acquired target-side translation policy?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Explicitation is another classic translation-universal claim.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** A pretrained LM can represent the intended relation without being forced to lexicalize it.  
**Ideal observable.** Hold recovered meaning fixed while manipulating whether explicit target realization is needed.  
**Best-case consequence.** Separate inference from realization in translation universals.  
**Headline.** `Explicitation reflects a realization policy, not extra semantic inference.`  
**Taste.** Active translationese/universals neighborhood; low independence.

### C4 — Normalization as target prior vs source fidelity
**Question.** Is normalization in LLM translation a consequence of strong target-language priors overwhelming source-marked forms?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Normalization is classically attributed to translation behavior; LLMs provide unusually strong pretrained target priors.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Modern training changes the load-bearing premise: target priors exist before translation supervision.  
**Ideal observable.** Same model, matched rare-but-valid target variants, varying source evidence and translation supervision.  
**Best-case consequence.** A quantitative law of source evidence vs pretrained target prior.  
**Headline.** `Translation normalization emerges when pretrained target priors outrun source evidence.`  
**Taste.** Strong shape, but owner pressure around translationese/SFT is already too close.

## Pool D — Path dependence, data order, developmental sequence

### D1 — Does language learning have a model-internal critical period?
**Question.** If the same linguistic evidence arrives late rather than early, is it learned differently because the model has crossed a representational/plasticity transition?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Critical-period accounts distinguish exposure timing from mature representational constraints.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Transformers can receive perfectly matched data in controlled sequences, unlike human studies.  
**Ideal observable.** Same cumulative data, delayed exposure, matched optimization budget, and direct representation/plasticity readout.  
**Best-case consequence.** Identify whether critical-period-like effects can arise from statistical learning alone.  
**Headline.** `Critical periods require an additional constraint beyond transformer statistical learning.`  
**Taste.** Direct TACL/ACL program already exists.

### D2 — Data order as selective inductive bias
**Question.** Does order merely speed convergence, or permanently change which linguistic regularities are represented?  
**Scientific ancestry.** SGD is path dependent; LLM pretraining usually treats corpus order as incidental/random.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Equal data and final loss can hide different learned representations.  
**Ideal observable.** Same multiset of examples and tokens, varied order only, checkpoint trajectories and matched downstream probes.  
**Best-case consequence.** Establish order as an independent source of model inductive bias.  
**Headline.** `Training order changes what a language model learns, not only how fast it learns it.`  
**Taste.** Direct 2026 owner program.

### D3 — Is there a universal easy→hard curriculum direction?
**Question.** Is curriculum direction a stable law or conditional on model capability and task structure?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Curriculum learning often assumes easy→hard is privileged.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Modern LMs already possess strong priors; `difficulty` is model-relative.  
**Ideal observable.** Crossed model capability × task complexity × multiple difficulty definitions.  
**Best-case consequence.** Replace a universal curriculum rule with a conditional law.  
**Headline.** `There is no universal curriculum direction; the useful order is capability- and task-dependent.`  
**Taste.** Direct ACL 2026 owner.

### D4 — Developmental order as cause of U-shaped linguistic behavior
**Question.** Are U-shaped acquisition trajectories caused by representational reorganization, or simply changing exposure/frequency over training?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Human and LM acquisition studies report U-shaped behavior in morphology/constructions.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Checkpoints permit direct alignment of behavior with internal representational change.  
**Ideal observable.** Same phenomenon tracked through training with controlled exposure statistics and state-level changes.  
**Best-case consequence.** Explain a developmental signature rather than merely reproducing it.  
**Headline.** `U-shaped acquisition marks a strategy transition rather than forgetting.`  
**Taste.** Too close to existing acquisition/training-dynamics programs and old representation-transition pools.

## Pool E — Formal succinctness and learnability

### E1 — Does succinct representability predict learnability?
**Question.** When a Transformer can encode a function exponentially more succinctly than an automaton/RNN, does gradient descent actually discover that succinct solution?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Expressivity does not imply trainability; new theory identifies succinctness as a different computational advantage.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** A representable compact circuit may still be inaccessible to optimization.  
**Ideal observable.** Formal task families with known description-size gaps, training trajectories, and recovered circuit complexity.  
**Best-case consequence.** Bridge formal expressivity to learned algorithm selection.  
**Headline.** `Transformers learn the succinct algorithms their theory predicts.`  
**Taste.** Directly owned by ACL Findings 2026.

### E2 — Succinctness as an inductive bias rather than capacity
**Question.** Does a Transformer prefer lower-description-complexity algorithms even when larger memorizing solutions fit training data equally well?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Succinctness theory is about representation size; grokking/MDL work asks which solution optimization selects.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** The same training accuracy can correspond to algorithmic or memorizing circuits.  
**Ideal observable.** Controlled equal-fit solutions with divergent description complexity and generalization.  
**Best-case consequence.** Establish a computational simplicity bias specific to attention architectures.  
**Headline.** `Attention turns representational succinctness into an optimization bias.`  
**Taste.** Still inside the just-opened succinctness→grokking program.

### E3 — Succinctness–verifiability tradeoff
**Question.** Does the same compactness that makes Transformers powerful make their learned computations intrinsically harder to verify?  
**Scientific ancestry.** ICLR 2026 proves succinct expression and EXPSPACE-hard verification are linked theoretically.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** It asks whether the theoretical by-product predicts practical model behavior/analysis difficulty.  
**Ideal observable.** Matched function families with controlled succinctness and verification/circuit-recovery complexity.  
**Best-case consequence.** A law relating representational compactness to interpretability/verification cost.  
**Headline.** `The source of Transformer computational efficiency is also a source of verification hardness.`  
**Taste.** Mainly a successor of the theory paper; reward upper bound unclear for ACL Main.

### E4 — Where does succinct computation first emerge in pretraining?
**Question.** Are compact algorithmic circuits formed abruptly at grokking-like transitions or gradually compressed from heuristics?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Learned algorithm formation and phase transitions are active questions.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Formal succinctness gives a claim-matched complexity coordinate for the circuit.  
**Ideal observable.** Circuit description complexity over checkpoints on tasks with a known succinct solution.  
**Best-case consequence.** Explain formation of abstract algorithms during training.  
**Headline.** `Algorithmic generalization begins when a heuristic circuit collapses into a succinct one.`  
**Taste.** Mechanistic successor cell, not new parent.

## Pool F — Event schemas and script knowledge

### F1 — Latent schema vs pairwise event association
**Question.** When a model predicts the next/omitted event in a familiar script, does it instantiate a reusable scenario-level schema or compose local pairwise event associations?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Script theory posits structured event knowledge; classical statistical script models often build pairwise/multi-relational event associations; LLMs now generate whole schemas.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Both accounts can predict frequent next events, but differ under recombination, omitted prerequisites, and global consistency.  
**Ideal observable.** A same-scenario intervention that changes a global schema variable while leaving local pairwise associations matched.  
**Best-case consequence.** Establish whether `script knowledge` in LMs is genuinely structured or a collection of local contingencies.  
**Headline.** `Language models predict scripted events without constructing a reusable event schema.` / opposite equally valuable.  
**Taste.** Good question; operation currently not identified.

### F2 — Causal prerequisite vs temporal adjacency in scripts
**Question.** Does the model treat an omitted prerequisite as inferable because of causal necessity or because it is statistically adjacent to the observed event?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Human script inference tolerates omitted causes but conflicts with explicit negation; LMs show a different pattern.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Causal and sequential associations are correlated in natural scripts.  
**Ideal observable.** Natural script pairs separating causal necessity from temporal frequency.  
**Best-case consequence.** Identify what relation underlies script completion.  
**Headline.** `LLM script inference tracks event association rather than causal prerequisites.`  
**Taste.** Very close to *SEM 2024 behavioral parent; mechanism route lacks unique operation.

### F3 — Hierarchical schema vs flat event graph
**Question.** Are coarse stages such as `ordering → eating → paying` represented as higher-order nodes controlling fine-grained events, or only inferred after the fact from a flat graph?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Event-schema induction explicitly outputs hierarchical relations; cognitive scripts posit nested structure.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Hierarchical text output does not prove hierarchical computation.  
**Ideal observable.** Selective manipulation of a coarse stage that transfers to multiple constituent events without direct local cues.  
**Best-case consequence.** Distinguish true hierarchical script organization from generated summaries.  
**Headline.** `Hierarchical event schemas are causal abstractions, not post-hoc labels.`  
**Taste.** Natural theory question, but current operation is generic causal-intervention machinery; weak `why 2026`.

### F4 — Schema persistence under event reordering
**Question.** Does a scenario representation survive unusual but coherent event order, or does the model rebuild the script from local sequential statistics?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Scripts encode typical progression but need not entail one rigid sequence.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Sequential LM probability strongly rewards canonical order.  
**Ideal observable.** Meaning-preserving reorderings with globally coherent scenario identity and controlled pair frequencies.  
**Best-case consequence.** Separate scenario identity from canonical event sequence.  
**Headline.** `Script identity survives order violations only when a global event model is maintained.`  
**Taste.** Risks becoming robustness/benchmark unless nested in F1.

## Pool G — Construction meaning, argument structure, semantic roles

### G1 — Construction meaning as reusable semantic operator
**Question.** Does a construction contribute a reusable semantic operation across novel verbs, or does the model reinterpret each verb–construction pairing independently?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Construction Grammar claims form–meaning pairings can contribute meaning beyond lexical items; caused-motion/coercion paradigms make this intuitive.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Successful novel-verb coercion can arise from lexical analogy without a shared constructional operator.  
**Ideal observable.** Cross-lexeme transfer of a constructional semantic state under controlled lexical semantics.  
**Best-case consequence.** Establish/refute a reusable construction-level computation.  
**Headline.** `Construction meaning is a reusable causal operator in language models.`  
**Taste.** Excellent plain-language question, but the parent is rapidly becoming an active program.

### G2 — Preemption vs entrenchment
**Question.** Does negative linguistic knowledge arise from a competing conventional form or merely from high overall verb frequency?  
**Scientific ancestry.** A classic Construction Grammar acquisition dispute.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** The two frequency signals are correlated observationally.  
**Ideal observable.** Controlled training intervention independently manipulating competing-form frequency and total verb frequency.  
**Best-case consequence.** Causally identify the acquisition mechanism.  
**Headline.** `Competing forms, not mere entrenchment, teach models what not to say.`  
**Taste.** Directly owned by 2026 work.

### G3 — Semantic role category vs distributed event-specific routing
**Question.** Are Agent/Patient roles reusable causal variables across predicates, or labels recoverable from predicate-specific circuits?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Thematic-role theories posit abstract roles; model work now localizes role information.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Decodability/localization does not imply a cross-predicate shared variable.  
**Ideal observable.** Cross-predicate causal interchange preserving lexical/event content.  
**Best-case consequence.** Determine whether abstract semantic roles are computational objects or analyst labels.  
**Headline.** `Semantic roles are reusable causal variables across predicates.`  
**Taste.** Too close to 2026 semantic-role causal-circuit program; `they localize, we interchange` would be prohibited salvage.

### G4 — Constructional semantics vs lexical event semantics
**Question.** When a novel construction changes a verb’s interpretation, which semantic content comes from the verb and which comes from the construction?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Long-standing lexicalist vs constructional accounts of argument structure; caused-motion and coercion are standard diagnostic objects.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Surface interpretation alone underdetermines source of meaning.  
**Ideal observable.** Independent manipulations of verb-root content and constructional event structure with transfer across novel pairings.  
**Best-case consequence.** Causal division-of-labor law between lexical and constructional semantics.  
**Headline.** `Argument-structure meaning factorizes into lexical content and constructional event structure.`  
**Taste.** Collapses into the same crowded CxG program unless a qualitatively new quantity appears.

## Pool H — Lexical event structure / verb-semantic decomposition

### H1 — Reusable event templates vs holistic verb representations
**Question.** Do modern autoregressive LMs compute verb meanings through reusable event-template variables such as ACT / CAUSE / BECOME that are shared across lexical roots, or is apparent decomposition implemented holistically per lexeme/context?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Event-structural theories of verb meaning explicitly posit a shared event template plus verb-specific root; this is a central theoretical decomposition, not an interpretability-made latent variable.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Behavioral entailments and linear decodability cannot distinguish a truly reusable causal abstraction from correlated holistic representations. Foundation models provide the possibility of a cross-lexeme causal abstraction test that human behavior cannot directly supply.  
**Ideal observable.** A theory-defined intervention/abstraction that selectively transfers or removes CAUSE/BECOME-like consequences across unrelated lexical roots while preserving root-specific manner/result content, and whose effects generalize across alternations rather than one prompt family.  
**Best-case consequence.** Establish or reject a computational analogue of one of lexical semantics’ most influential decomposition claims.  
**Headline A.** `Verb meaning in language models factorizes into reusable event templates and lexeme-specific roots.`  
**Headline B.** `Apparent verb-semantic decomposition is not implemented through reusable event primitives.`  
**Taste.** **PRESSURE SURVIVOR — no L, no pilot.**

### H2 — Causative/inchoative alternation as nested-template test
**Question.** For alternators such as `break`, does the model reuse one BECOME subevent inside a larger CAUSE(BECOME(...)) representation, or learn two related lexical mappings?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Event-structure theory predicts literal nesting/shared substructure across the alternation.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Similar embeddings or behavioral transfer do not identify substructure reuse.  
**Ideal observable.** A selective operation on the shared change-of-state component with parallel consequences in inchoative and causative uses, while CAUSE-specific behavior changes only in the transitive form.  
**Best-case consequence.** Direct evidence for nested semantic computation rather than similarity.  
**Headline.** `Causative alternations reuse a shared change-of-state computation.`  
**Taste.** Best viewed as an identification substrate for H1, not a separate candidate.

### H3 — Template vs root as mediator of grammar
**Question.** Is shared event template actually the causal mediator linking verb meaning to argument realization, or do root-specific semantics directly drive grammar?  
**Scientific ancestry.** A core event-structure claim is that templates largely determine grammatical properties, while recent lexical-semantic work shows roots can contribute broader eventive information than strict bifurcation predicts.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** Modern theory itself disputes the division of labor; the model offers a system in which semantic variables and grammatical consequences can potentially be manipulated together.  
**Ideal observable.** Change template-level event semantics while preserving root identity, then test coordinated changes in entailments and argument realization; complementary root manipulation should preserve template-driven grammar if the strong account is right.  
**Best-case consequence.** A revised computational division of labor between abstract event structure and lexical content.  
**Headline.** `The semantic variable that predicts argument structure is not purely the event template; root information enters the causal path.`  
**Taste.** Scientifically stronger than H2, but shares H1’s identification blocker.

### H4 — Manner/result complementarity as representational law
**Question.** Is the classical manner–result tradeoff reflected in mutually exclusive representational allocation, or is it only a lexical-distribution regularity with both dimensions simultaneously represented?  
**Scientific ancestry.** Manner/Result Complementarity has generated decades of theory and counterexamples; modern work increasingly treats the distinction as graded/violable.  
**Why non-autocomplete.** A language model trained on usage could represent both dimensions even if the surface lexicon prefers one.  
**Ideal observable.** Same-verb/context decomposition of manner and result consequences with independently manipulable event-template/root content.  
**Best-case consequence.** Clarify whether a famous lexical-semantic regularity is a representational constraint or a distributional tendency.  
**Headline.** `Manner/result complementarity does not reflect representational exclusivity.`  
**Taste.** Potentially useful boundary inside H1; not independent enough for a second parent.

# 4. Owner / construct assassination of the strongest eight

## 4.1 A1 — native probabilistic generation — **K246**

Direct owners now form a sequence:

- Gu et al., COLING 2025, **Do LLMs Play Dice?** — explicit/implicit probability distributions; models understand probabilities but struggle to sample from them.
- Guo et al., Findings EMNLP 2025, **The Illusion of Randomness** — explicitly names a cognition–behaviour gap: models articulate the Nash distribution yet choices systematically deviate; investigates positional bias.
- Zhao et al., ACL 2026, **Large Language Models Are Bad Dice Players** — large-scale audit across 11 models/15 distributions and batch vs independent protocols.

The mechanism residual is attractive, but under this repository’s rules it compresses to `three papers establish cognition/sampling gap → we mechanistically explain it`, which is exactly the forbidden `behavior owner → mechanism salvage` pattern in an already active object.

**Verdict:** K246.

## 4.2 B1/B2 — factual recall architecture / geometry — **K247**

- Choe et al., EMNLP 2025, **Do All Autoregressive Transformers Remember Facts the Same Way?** directly establishes cross-architecture variation: Qwen-like models show unusually strong early-attention contribution relative to the established MLP story.
- Ravfogel et al., 2026, **Geometric Factual Recall in Transformers** develops and trains an explicit alternative to pairwise associative memory: embeddings encode superpositions of attributes and an MLP acts as a reusable relation-conditioned selector.
- Yan & Jia, EMNLP 2025, **Promote, Suppress, Iterate** already decomposes multi-answer factual recall mechanistically.

A hidden-condition law would be valuable, but at present the proposed question is generated by placing these new papers next to one another. No independent architecture quantity predicts the mechanism before the owner papers are read.

**Verdict:** K247.

## 4.3 C1/C2 — translation universals in the LLM regime — **K248**

- Li et al., ACL 2025, **Lost in Literalism** already roots LLM translationese in supervised translation training and intervenes on training data.
- Jiang, 2025, **Does LLM translation align with translation universals?** directly compares LLM vs human translation on simplification across genres.
- Adjacent 2025–2026 work studies translationese indices, explicitation, normalization and simplification for LLM translation.

The modern regime does break a classical premise, but the community is already actively rebuilding the law under that regime. A new universal/cue is a cell, not a fresh parent.

**Verdict:** K248.

## 4.4 D1–D3 — data order / critical periods — **K249**

This parent is now unmistakably active:

- TACL 2025 critical-period work tests delayed L2 exposure and whether statistical learning alone produces CP effects.
- ACL/EACL 2026 work systematically studies curriculum/data organization in pretraining and fine-tuning.
- 2026 cross-architecture work reports architecture-dependent effects of pretraining data order.
- ACL 2026 **What Makes a Good Curriculum?** already replaces a universal easy→hard story with capability/task-dependent conditions.

The attractive headline `same data, different order → different learned system` is no longer an unasked law.

## 4.8 H1 — reusable event templates + lexical roots — **PRESSURE SURVIVOR, NOT A CANDIDATE**

### Why this pressure is different

The scientific object exists independently of language models and independently of any interpretability method. Event-structural theories explicitly decompose verb meaning into:

- a **shared event template** expressing broad temporal/causal structure, built from primitives such as ACT / CAUSE / BECOME;
- an **idiosyncratic lexical root** carrying verb-specific state/action content.

The strongest form additionally claims that the template is the main semantic correlate of regular grammatical properties such as argument realization.

Recent lexical-semantic work is still revising the template/root division of labor rather than treating it as settled: root meanings can contribute change/causation information, manner/result complementarity has important counterexamples, and cross-linguistic evidence motivates richer interactions.

### Why the 2026 model regime might genuinely change the inference

Human behavioral experiments can show that event-template complexity affects processing or that classes differ in syntax/semantics, but they cannot directly test whether **the same internal computation** for CAUSE/BECOME is reused across unrelated lexical roots.

A foundation model potentially permits a stronger question:

> If two verbs share the same theorized event template but differ completely in lexical root, is there a causal abstraction whose manipulation transfers the template-defined semantic and grammatical consequences across those roots while leaving root-specific meaning intact?

That is not `does the model know change-of-state verbs?` and not `can we decode CAUSE?`. It is an explicit **causal-abstraction / factorization claim** derived from the linguistic theory itself.

### Why it is not yet L39

The operation is not secured. Ordinary activation patching, a learned steering vector, or a probe-defined CAUSE direction would be circular or method-first. Before Selection, the project needs a theory-defined operation/abstraction test with:

1. independent semantic consequences of template content and root content;
2. cross-lexeme transfer that is meaningful even when surface tokens/syntax differ;
3. active controls showing root-specific meaning is preserved;
4. a bridge from intervention effect to **shared computation**, not merely shared features;
5. owner search expanded beyond the exact phrases already queried, including lexical-semantics/model-mechanism work outside ACL venues.

**Current status:** `PRESSURE SURVIVOR — IDENTIFICATION BLOCKER — NO L NUMBER — NO PILOT`.
