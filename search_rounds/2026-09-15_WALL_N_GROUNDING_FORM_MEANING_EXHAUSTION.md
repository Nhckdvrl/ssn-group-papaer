# 1. Mother problem

> **What aspects of meaning can be identified from relations among linguistic forms alone, and what requires a link to extra-linguistic states, perception, action, or reference?**

This is older than LLMs. Harnad's symbol-grounding problem, distributional semantics, conceptual-role semantics, formal semantics, and later debates about text-only learning all instantiate different versions of the same scientific dispute.

Important anchors:
- Harnad 1990, *The Symbol Grounding Problem*.
- Bender & Koller, ACL 2020 Best Theme, *Climbing towards NLU: On Meaning, Form, and Understanding in the Age of Data*.
- Merrill et al., TACL 2021, *Provable Limitations of Acquiring Meaning from Ungrounded Form*.
- Piantadosi & Hill 2022, *Meaning without reference in large language models*.
- Gubelmann et al., EMNLP 2024, *Pragmatic Norms Are All You Need—Why the Symbol Grounding Problem Does Not Apply to LLMs*.

The dispute is real: one lineage argues that form-only evidence leaves important semantic/reference distinctions unidentified; another argues that conceptual/inferential role realized in linguistic practice can itself constitute substantial meaning.

# 2. Modern multimodality is a real regime change, but not an empty surface

Multimodal and embodied models make it possible to expose the same linguistic form to additional perceptual/action evidence. This changes the evidence available to the learner, but it does not automatically create a new scientific question.

The obvious descendants are already direct programs:
- whether visual grounding improves embodied/world knowledge;
- whether multimodal models resolve ambiguities text-only models cannot;
- where grounded concepts appear internally;
- how language priors compete with visual evidence;
- whether the model follows a counterfactual image when it contradicts memorized world knowledge.

Representative current work includes CL 2024 discussions of multimodal grounding, 2025 embodied-knowledge studies, and mechanistic work on grounding emergence.

# 3. Strongest attempted residual: conflict between conceptual role and reference

A sharper test would deliberately place linguistic/world priors in conflict with perceptual evidence and ask whether the model's semantic decision follows the textual conceptual role or the current grounded referent.

This initially appeared attractive because the old theories seem to privilege different evidence sources.

It does not survive ownership.

EMNLP 2025 *Pixels Versus Priors* directly constructs counterfactual visual evidence that conflicts with memorized priors and follows the competition across layers. ICML 2025 and 2026 AAAI/CVPR work now study modality conflict and cross-modal inconsistency as explicit scientific objects.

Therefore the residual reviewer-compresses to:

> `old grounding debate + current language-prior-vs-visual-evidence conflict paradigm`.

# 4. Why no candidate survives

- `text-only vs multimodal semantics` is a model-family comparison, not a new inference.
- `where grounding lives internally` is behavior → mechanism / interpretability sequel.
- `visual evidence vs language prior` is directly owned.
- `does grounding improve concept X` becomes another benchmark/competence cell.
- a philosophical statement that reference is non-identifiable from text alone is already formalized in prior theory; demonstrating one more ambiguity does not create a Main-level contribution.

# 5. Decision

Keep the durable distinction:

> **distributional/conceptual-role information and intervention/reference-grounded information are different evidential objects; adding a modality does not by itself identify meaning.**

Do not regenerate text-vs-vision comparisons, grounding probes, language-prior conflict, or another conceptual phenomenon × VLM test without a new theory-specific quantity.
