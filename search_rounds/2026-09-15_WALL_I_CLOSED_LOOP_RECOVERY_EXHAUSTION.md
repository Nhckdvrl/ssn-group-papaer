# 0. Why this wall was opened

The search deliberately left the vocabulary of WALL-D/F/G/H, lexical-event-structure, and the current IP03 succinctness–learnability route.

The durable question considered here is older than foundation models:

> **A sequence predictor is trained mainly on histories generated by the data / expert process, but at deployment it recursively conditions on its own outputs. When a rollout deviates, what determines whether the deviation amplifies, persists, or is absorbed by the subsequent dynamics?**

This is not initially an LLM question. It appears in:

- teacher forcing and scheduled sampling;
- sequential prediction and imitation learning;
- dynamical-system stability;
- autoregressive text generation;
- later, multi-turn LLM interaction and self-correction.

The attraction of the wall is a genuine historical tension: the standard exposure-bias story predicts compounding distortion, while direct language-generation experiments reported surprisingly strong **self-recovery**.

The wall therefore passed the admission test:

> delete all 2025–2026 papers and the question still matters.

Candidate generation remained closed while the lineage was reconstructed.

# 1. Seminal formulation: training on expert histories vs rolling out on self-generated histories

## 1.1 Scheduled Sampling — the canonical compounding-error story

Bengio et al. (NeurIPS 2015), **Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks**, formulate the train/test mismatch directly: during training, sequence models receive the previous ground-truth token, while inference conditions on the model's own previous prediction. A mistake can therefore move the model to a history it did not train on, increasing the chance of further mistakes.

Source:
- https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/hash/e995f98d56967d946471af29d7bf99f1-Abstract.html

The scientific claim hidden inside the engineering motivation is stronger than merely `train and test distributions differ`:

> **errors produced under rollout change the future state distribution in a direction that makes subsequent prediction progressively worse.**

That is a dynamical claim.

## 1.2 Sequence-level training inherits the same diagnosis

Ranzato et al. (2015), **Sequence Level Training with Recurrent Neural Networks**, similarly motivate sequence-level optimization by the discrepancy between teacher-forced training and autoregressive deployment, where prediction errors can accumulate.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06732

This established a long-lived default intuition:

> good local prediction under expert prefixes is not enough; closed-loop generation can drift.

---

# 2. Early critique: even the standard repair does not identify the underlying problem

Huszár (2015), **How (not) to Train your Generative Model: Scheduled Sampling, Likelihood, Adversary?**, shows that scheduled sampling does not optimize a proper likelihood-like objective and can be statistically inconsistent.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05101

This matters for the wall because it separates two claims that were often bundled:

1. self-generated histories may be difficult;
2. therefore mixing self-generated tokens into training is the correct remedy.

The second does not follow from the first.

Later work such as Professor Forcing (Lamb et al., NeurIPS 2016) attacks the train/free-running mismatch at the level of hidden dynamics rather than simply replacing observed tokens.

Source:
- https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/16026d60ff9b54410b3435b403afd226-Abstract.html

Already by this point, `exposure bias` is not one mechanism. It is a family of claims about off-distribution states, rollout dynamics, model generalization, and training objectives.

# 3. The key failed prediction: text-generation distortion need not accumulate

## 3.1 Schmidt 2019 — exposure bias should be treated as a generalization problem

Schmidt (NGT 2019), **Generalization in Generation: A closer look at Exposure Bias**, argues that the relevant object is generalization from observed to generated contexts rather than exposure bias as an automatic pathology of teacher forcing.

Source:
- https://aclanthology.org/D19-5616/

This weakens the inference:

> `self-generated prefix` → necessarily pathological state.

A model can in principle generalize well to generated histories.

## 3.2 He et al. EMNLP 2021 — direct contradiction to monotone accumulation

He, Zhang, Zhou & Glass, **Exposure Bias versus Self-Recovery: Are Distortions Really Incremental for Autoregressive Text Generation?**, directly measure how generated rather than ground-truth prefixes affect open-ended generation.

Source:
- https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.415/

Their result is precisely the kind of historical friction this repository wants to preserve:

- the prefix discrepancy does hurt generation;
- but the distortion is **limited**;
- it does **not appear to increase incrementally** with continued generation;
- models exhibit a surprising **self-recovery** effect, including after strongly corrupted prefixes.

This is not a new benchmark cell. It directly challenges the load-bearing dynamical prediction behind the usual compounding-error story.

### SAME-QUANTITY status

At the qualitative level, the disagreement is real:

> deviation in rollout history → future predictive / generation degradation as horizon increases.

But the literatures do not yet all instantiate the exact same observable. Exposure-bias arguments often reason about sequence error / occupancy drift, whereas He et al. quantify open-ended text quality, diversity, and consistency. This distinction becomes important below.

---

# 4. A second mature explanation: unexpected contexts produce structured generalization

Bau & Andreas, EMNLP 2021, **How Do Neural Sequence Models Generalize? Local and Global Cues for Out-of-Distribution Prediction**, ask what happens after a sequence model encounters an unexpected token.

Source:
- https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.448/

They find that RNNs and Transformers do not respond arbitrarily. Their next-token distributions interpolate in a structured way between:

- a local-context predictor consistent with the recent token;
- a global-context predictor consistent with broader sequence structure.

They additionally show that different kinds of noise shift this balance and provide a preliminary feature-correlation account.

This directly occupies one tempting explanation for He-style self-recovery:

> `the model ignores a bad local token and snaps back to global context`.

The idea is not unowned. More importantly, Bau & Andreas show that recovery is unlikely to be one scalar property: behavior depends on the relation between the perturbation and the global structure of the sequence.

---

# 5. Modern correction: autoregressive inference and teacher-forced learning are distinct failure modes

Bachmann & Nagarajan, ICML 2024, **The Pitfalls of Next-Token Prediction**, explicitly separate:

1. **autoregressive-inference error accumulation**; and
2. **teacher-forcing failure to learn the correct next-token predictor at all**.

Source:
- https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/bachmann24a.html

Their minimal planning task shows that teacher forcing can enable a model to fit local targets without learning the lookahead computation required for the task, even before any rollout-distribution-shift argument is invoked.

This is important anti-confusion state for WALL-I:

> A long-horizon failure after next-token training is not automatically evidence of compounding rollout errors.

It can originate earlier, because the one-step conditional learned under teacher forcing is itself the wrong computational object for the task.

Therefore any future candidate would need to establish first that the reference next-token predictor is accurate under relevant prefixes before interpreting rollout degradation as a stability phenomenon.

# 6. Cross-field ancestry: compounding error already has a mature `recoverability` theory

This is the most damaging finding for novelty.

The sequence-generation story is a special case of a much older sequential-decision / imitation-learning problem:

> behavior cloning is trained on expert-induced states but deployment induces learner states; prediction error can change the future state distribution and compound.

DAgger-style work attacks exactly this distribution-shift problem by collecting labels on learner-induced states.

But the modern imitation-learning literature goes further than simply saying `distribution shift exists`.

## 6.1 Swamy et al. ICML 2021 — **moment recoverability**

**Of Moments and Matching: A Game-Theoretic Framework for Closing the Imitation Gap** introduces **moment recoverability**, a property that cleanly controls how strongly families of imitation-learning methods can mitigate compounding errors.

Source:
- https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/swamy21a.html

This is almost exactly the explanatory level that initially looked open for language:

> what property of a sequential system decides whether a local deviation creates an ever-growing long-horizon loss or can be recovered from?

The generic quantity is already a mature theoretical object.

## 6.2 Stability / recovery as explicit closed-loop dynamics

Recent control-theoretic behavior-cloning work derives conditions under which learner trajectories converge back toward demonstrated behavior rather than drifting farther away.

Representative source:
- **Stable-BC: Controlling Covariate Shift with Stable Behavior Cloning** (Mehta et al., RA-L 2025): https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06246

Conversely, Simchowitz, Pfrommer & Jadbabaie (COLT 2025), **The Pitfalls of Imitation Learning when Actions are Continuous**, show that even systems with stable underlying dynamics can exhibit execution error exponentially larger than error measured on expert-distributed training states under important policy classes.

Source:
- https://proceedings.mlr.press/v291/simchowitz25a.html

The 2025 follow-up on mitigating compounding error formalizes incremental stability / recovery conditions and interventions such as action chunking or noise injection.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09061

### Consequence for our search

A proposed language paper whose core move is:

> define a recovery coefficient / contraction coefficient and explain when autoregressive errors compound

reviewer-compresses to:

> **He et al. self-recovery + imitation-learning / control recoverability, instantiated for text.**

That is exactly the prohibited `Prior A + Prior B` shape.

# 7. Foundation-model regime evidence does not rescue the parent

## 7.1 TACL 2024 self-correction — active correction is a different object, already dense

Kamoi et al., **When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes?**, critically survey LLM self-correction.

Source:
- https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/

They find that prompted intrinsic self-correction is not reliably established in general tasks; reliable external feedback or substantial fine-tuning can enable correction, and feedback generation is often the bottleneck.

This is **not the same quantity** as passive autoregressive self-recovery: self-correction adds an explicit evaluate/revise operation. But it occupies the natural descendant:

> `modern LLMs can notice a wrong trajectory and recover from it`.

Any candidate that adds reflection, verifier feedback, critique, or retry becomes self-correction research rather than a clean test of WALL-I.

## 7.2 ICLR 2026 Best Paper — multi-turn LLMs often do not recover

Laban et al., **LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation**, find a large performance gap between fully specified single-turn tasks and underspecified multi-turn tasks. Their analysis attributes much of the loss to unreliability: models make premature assumptions and over-rely on earlier mistaken solutions rather than recovering later.

Sources:
- https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/59f6421e64707225fdf5b28840679a07-Abstract-Conference.html
- https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/llms-get-lost-in-multi-turn-conversation/

This superficially conflicts with He-style self-recovery, but **fails SAME-QUANTITY**:

- He et al.: perturbation / generated-prefix discrepancy inside autoregressive open-ended text generation;
- Laban et al.: information arriving over interactive turns, premature task commitment, and later task completion reliability.

Multi-turn failure is evidence that recovery is not universal, not a direct contradiction on one estimand.

Turning WALL-I into `why do old LMs self-recover but modern chat models get lost?` would therefore require inventing a bridge after seeing the two papers. That is not allowed.

# 8. Strongest residual candidate — and why it fails reviewer compression

After lineage saturation, the strongest candidate-shaped residual was:

> **What structural condition makes a self-generated deviation contract rather than amplify under autoregressive rollout?**

A natural decisive object would be a **perturbation-response curve**:

- hold the model and initial prefix fixed;
- inject one controlled deviation at time `t`;
- measure the effect on the same task-relevant state / distribution at horizons `t+k`;
- ask whether the effect decays, persists, or grows.

This is much better than `measure exposure bias on modern LLMs`, because both signs would matter and the quantity is dynamic.

However it still fails promotion.

## 8.1 Reviewer compression

The strongest reviewer says:

> `He et al. 2021 already discovered self-recovery after prefix corruption. Bau & Andreas already characterize prediction after unexpected tokens. Imitation-learning theory already says compounding depends on recoverability / stability. You are measuring that same old recovery property in larger language models.`

That compression is successful.

## 8.2 `Natural language redundancy causes recovery` does not save it

A tempting explanation is that broad linguistic / semantic context is redundant enough to override one local deviation.

But this mostly restates the global-context account already directly studied by Bau & Andreas. It also turns into generic noise robustness unless a mature linguistic theory gives two opposite predictions about the *same* perturbation.

No such unowned theory-specific contrast was found in this audit.

## 8.3 `Reasoning errors are different from text errors` does not save it

Another tempting move is:

> ordinary text is recoverable but reasoning / planning contains irreversible fork points.

This is scientifically plausible, but current ownership is severe:

- ICML 2024 already separates planning failures of next-token prediction from ordinary autoregressive rhetoric;
- self-correction / verifier literature directly studies error localization and recovery in reasoning;
- current work on branching / forking tokens and diversity makes token criticality itself an active object.

Without an independently old theory fixing the recoverable-vs-irreversible state variable, this becomes a post-hoc task taxonomy.

## 8.4 `One scalar recovery coefficient` is scientifically suspect

Bau & Andreas already show response to unexpected contexts depends on local/global cue structure. Imitation learning distinguishes reward-, value-, and dynamics-dependent forms of recoverability. LLM self-correction depends on whether reliable feedback exists.

So there is no evidence that one domain-independent scalar is the natural scientific object.

Inventing such a metric would risk precisely the evaluator/quantity-first failure that the current search doctrine forbids.

# 9. Failed-prediction map

| historical claim | later pressure | current inference |
|---|---|---|
| self-generated history necessarily causes progressively worse generation | He et al. 2021 find limited, non-incremental distortion + self-recovery | monotone compounding is not a universal law of text generation |
| generated context is simply pathological OOD input | Schmidt 2019 + Bau/Andreas 2021 show structured generalization | generated / unexpected context must be analyzed through generalization structure, not a binary train/test label |
| long-horizon failure after teacher forcing diagnoses exposure bias | Bachmann & Nagarajan 2024 show teacher-forcing can fail before rollout | learning failure and rollout instability must be separated |
| recoverability is an unexplained language-specific mystery | imitation-learning / control theory explicitly formalizes recoverability/stability | generic recovery law is already a mature cross-field object |
| modern LLM self-correction can supply the missing mechanism | TACL 2024 shows correction depends heavily on feedback; ICLR 2026 shows interactive unreliability | active correction / interaction are neighboring but distinct, already occupied regimes |

# 10. Anti-resurrection additions

Until genuinely new leverage appears, do **not** regenerate:

- `exposure bias in modern LLMs`;
- `do errors compound in chain-of-thought?` as a raw behavioral survey;
- `measure self-recovery after corrupting a prefix`;
- a generic `recovery coefficient`, `contraction score`, or `error half-life`;
- teacher-forced vs free-running hidden-state distance as the paper;
- scheduled-sampling / on-policy-distillation reruns whose contribution is only better robustness;
- `He 2021 but with reasoning / instruction tuning / newer models`;
- `He 2021 self-recovery + imitation-learning recoverability`;
- `old self-recovery vs ICLR 2026 multi-turn lostness` without a SAME-QUANTITY bridge fixed independently of the papers;
- reflection/verifier/self-critique variants, which enter the already dense self-correction parent.

A future reopening requires something qualitatively stronger:

> **two mature theories of a natural sequential-language state must make opposite predictions for the same predeclared perturbation-response quantity under one controlled treatment, or a genuinely new operation must identify a distinction that recoverability theory could not previously identify.**

New model size, reasoning traces, prompt formats, or a new error taxonomy are insufficient.

# 11. Boundary against existing repository state

## Not WALL-H / reachable support

WALL-H asks which behaviors remain accessible under a resource/intervention class. WALL-I asks how a **single rollout state evolves after deviation**. A behavior can remain reachable globally while one local trajectory is dynamically unstable.

## Not WALL-E / L40 developmental state

WALL-E asks whether different past learning histories leave a latent state that changes the response to the **next learning event**. WALL-I holds weights fixed and asks how inference-time rollout state responds to a perturbation. No curriculum / future weight update is required.

## Not IP10 / generic multi-turn reliability

IP10 concerns capability deployment across interaction. WALL-I is narrower and older: closed-loop sequential prediction under self-generated state distribution. Multi-turn evidence may bear on the wall only after SAME-QUANTITY matching.

## Not temporal forgetting

No claim concerns stored knowledge being erased vs suppressed over training time.

# 12. Wall verdict

Scientific value of the durable problem:

- **naturalness:** high;
- **ancestry:** deep and explicit;
- **failed prediction:** real;
- **modern relevance:** very high;
- **current search headroom:** low.

The key negative discovery is itself useful:

> **The missing explanation is not simply `why do language models sometimes recover?`; sequential-learning theory already has recovery/stability as a first-class variable. A Main-level language paper would need a language-specific theory that predicts a different recovery structure, not merely a new measurement of the generic one.**

**Decision: WALL-I exhausted as a current generator. No new L-series. No pilot.**
