# Research Topic Selection: Question, Idea, Contribution

Updated: 2026-09-11. Target: ACL / EMNLP / NAACL Main. Strong Main and award papers are calibration references, not templates or acceptance guarantees.

This document decides whether a candidate deserves a bounded investigation and, later, a full study. Search is governed by [RESEARCH_TOPIC_SEARCH.md](RESEARCH_TOPIC_SEARCH.md); execution by [RESEARCH_EXECUTION.md](RESEARCH_EXECUTION.md).

## 1. Evaluate Three Different Objects

| Object | What we need | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| Research question | A natural, consequential uncertainty, understandable without naming our tools | An interesting parent paper or fashionable model |
| Explanatory idea / research operation | A way to change the answer, distinguish accounts, or expose a consequential missing variable | A probe, patch, subspace, new title, or attractive metaphor |
| Paper contribution | A result that changes understanding or practice beyond prior work | A good RQ plus several successful experiments |

Evaluate these separately. A strong question does not license every proposed answer. A good idea need not have a complete mechanism before discovery. A candidate can have an excellent RQ and still lack a viable paper contribution.

Do not demand that a pilot prove the paper in advance. Demand a credible contribution path, with uncertainties stated.

## 2. Promotion States Have Limited Meaning

- **ROUGH LEAD:** interesting question; contribution path undeveloped.
- **SERIOUS CANDIDATE:** worth source/data/idea investigation, not substantial compute.
- **PILOT-AUTHORIZED:** permission for a named, bounded experiment resolving a specified uncertainty. Not approval of the paper identity or full-study expansion.
- **GO-TO-FULL-STUDY:** pilot evidence, current novelty, and identification justify a claim-linked depth/breadth program.
- **HOLD:** a named blocking issue, a bounded way to decide it, and an explicit review point. Not indefinite preservation.
- **RECONSTRUCT:** a genuinely different explanation or contribution needs evaluation.
- **ARCHIVED / NO-GO:** stop the current route; preserve evidence and rationale. This need not mean the entire RQ is unanswerable or all neighboring ideas are owned.

Directory location, previous approval, compute expenditure, and experiment count confer no status.

## 3. Start with a Natural Tension

Give a plain example. Explain why a knowledgeable reader cannot confidently infer the answer, or why a known answer has a consequential untested scope.

State the best existing explanation before proposing ours. What would ours explain, predict, distinguish, or enable that it does not?

Multiple plausible accounts help, but do not mechanically invent A/B/C. A measurement, system principle, resource, or empirical law can also anchor a paper. Do not force all NLP research into a mechanistic-interpretability template.

An established anomaly lowers phenomenon risk. It does not establish that its remaining explanation space is novel, identifiable, or important. Discovering a new phenomenon is also allowed when the search and validation have a principled basis.

## 4. Audit Ownership in Both Directions

Read the closest primary papers, including relevant experiments, limitations, and versions. Distinguish demonstrated results, authors' interpretations, and our inferences. Verify publication/award status rather than inheriting it from discussion summaries.

For each proposed paper identity write:

1. Its central scientific statement, without incidental model/dataset/layer names.
2. The closest works and exactly what they establish.
3. The strongest honest compression: **Prior Work A + B + C = our paper**.
4. The strongest surviving contribution: what does that combination fail to explain or imply?
5. Why the remainder changes understanding or a meaningful NLP decision.

**Component overlap is not a fatal collision. Component combination is not a contribution by itself.** A new relationship between known objects can be important; require a reason it was unresolved and evidence that can establish it. Do not reject it merely because every noun has appeared before.

Use distinct judgments:

- **Direct collision:** prior work establishes the central answer and consequence at the relevant scope.
- **Insufficient contribution:** no exact owner is established, but the remainder is obvious, minor, or unmotivated.
- **Unresolved novelty:** source verification or the distinction remains unclear.
- **Plausible independent contribution:** a meaningful unresolved statement and credible resolving operation remain.

Do not call the second or third case proof of the first. Removing every known supporting fact is not the test: ask whether the paper's new inference is substantial.

## 5. The Successful-Result Test Comes Before Scaling

Write the strongest plausible positive result, then ask:

> Even if we obtain this spectacular result, what exactly would it establish, and what simpler explanation would still survive?

Trace **observation -> measured quantity -> inference -> contribution**. Identify the weakest arrow.

Examples:

- Decodable information need not control a decision.
- A late state transplant changing an answer does not alone identify the computation that formed it; answer-code transfer can suffice.
- A same-answer cross-format transplant leaving output unchanged does not alone establish interchangeability; it may be an ineffective intervention.
- Two presentations of one generating process need not provide the same observed evidence.
- Checkpoint siblings, sequential training stages, and same-weight inference modes answer different attribution questions.

These are examples, not a compulsory control battery. Choose the smallest comparison separating live alternatives. If success cannot support the interesting claim, redesign the operation before collecting more results.

## 6. Make the Contribution Path Visible

Sketch how the initial result could develop into an explanation, generalization, meaningful boundary, consequence, or useful capability. Each step should deepen the same contribution or reveal a better one, not merely add another technique.

Do not require exactly three headline claims. Known substrate results and necessary controls can remain supporting evidence. Surprise matters when it changes understanding; an expected result can matter when it resolves consequential uncertainty.

Record informative outcomes, including outcomes that end the route. Not every sign must yield a paper. Do not promise to reinterpret every failure as success.

## 7. Calibrate the Intellectual Advance and Workload

Before substantial authorization, compare against the strongest relevant Main papers. Normally use three complementary anchors: a problem neighbor, an evidence/operation neighbor, and a contribution-depth neighbor. Explain a missing match. Use award papers when pertinent; use ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS for technical ownership without losing the NLP contribution.

Record in existing candidate documents:

| Comparison | Required question |
|---|---|
| Intellectual advance | What did readers learn beyond prior work? What is our counterpart? |
| Decisive inference | Which experiment separated important explanations? Does ours do so? |
| Development | What moved the paper beyond its first finding? What comparable inference is missing here? |
| Breadth | What independent units, domains, model/training axes support the scope? Which do we need and why? |
| Consequence | What changed because of the finding? Is ours independent or just restated C1? |
| Work remaining | What concrete work closes the largest gap? What would make it not worth doing? |

Do not write only "hypothesis -> probe -> patch -> intervention, therefore aligned." Do not copy model counts. Exact experimental calibration requires methods/results and relevant appendices, not only abstracts.

Separate a **pilot budget** from a **full-study burden**. The latter may require substantial independent stimuli, external validation, competing mechanisms, and consequences. More samples on three prompts do not supply population breadth. A general mechanism claim may need another domain; a domain-specific claim may need deeper coverage within its natural domain. Neither two domains nor eighteen models is a universal requirement.

## 8. Compact Authorization Record

Keep this in the candidate package, not another root checklist:

1. RQ, example, and why the unresolved answer matters.
2. Proposed idea/operation and what the strongest existing explanation misses.
3. Current ownership audit, best compression, and surviving contribution.
4. Data provenance, independent units, and successful-result inference test.
5. Named pilot, possible outcomes, and stop/reconstruct conditions.
6. Main-paper comparisons and likely full-study depth/breadth burden.
7. Verdict, authorization scope, and unresolved risks.

Fatal known problems block promotion. Unknowns the pilot is designed to resolve need not already be solved. After the pilot, evaluate the current contribution rather than inheriting original approval.

## 9. L12 Lesson for Future Selection

L12 illustrates a failure of research decisions, not a ban on reasoning, causal analysis, or risky-choice research. A natural RQ and strong local effects were allowed to stand in for an independently valuable explanatory idea. Increasingly abstract names then risked upgrading the same evidence without a new discriminating operation.

The opposite mistake is to list adjacent papers and declare every possible synthesis owned. Identify either an actual collision or a substantive reason the remaining contribution is inadequate. See the [execution retrospective](RESEARCH_EXECUTION.md#12-l12-retrospective-what-the-workflow-missed).

**Authorize learning at pilot scale; authorize a paper program only when its current contribution deserves that investment.**
