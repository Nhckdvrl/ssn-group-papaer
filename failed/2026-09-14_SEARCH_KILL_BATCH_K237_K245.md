## K244 — Bridging relation provenance: stored world association vs discourse-built relation

**Status:** KILL  
**Primary failure:** `IDENTIFICATION / CONSTRUCT FAILURE`  
**Secondary failure:** `REVIEWER_COMPRESSION`

The question looked strong because ordinary bridging accuracy cannot reveal where the relation came from. On selection audit, however, the two proposed provenance sources cannot be cleanly manipulated while preserving the same scientific quantity.

- A knowledge edit intended to remove `house -> door` can also change the lexical/world semantics that make the bridge meaningful.
- Replacing familiar entities/relations with nonce entities removes the parametric source entirely and changes the question into in-context relation learning.
- Explicitly contradicting world knowledge in context collapses toward the generic `parametric prior vs context evidence` conflict, with bridging serving mainly as the task wrapper.

Therefore the hoped-for `relation-provenance conditional law` is not identified pre-result. A beautiful regime switch would not repair the construct problem.

**Reviewer compression:** “context-vs-parametric knowledge conflict, instantiated on bridging.”

**Do not reopen by:** a different bridge subtype, stronger knowledge editing, entity replacement, language/model changes, or a denser prior-strength sweep. Reopen only if a genuinely same-quantity intervention independently changes relation provenance while preserving the referential relation and lexical semantics.

## K245 — Focus alternatives as a shared causal latent object

**Status:** KILL CURRENT FORM  
**Primary failure:** `REAL_OBJECT / IDENTIFICATION FAILURE`  
**Secondary failure:** `PAPER_SCALE_FAILURE`

The attractive version asks whether one operator-independent alternative-set object is reused across exclusion, contrast, reference, and multiple focus-sensitive operators. That is stronger than locating a `focus feature`, but the current textual setting does not provide a clean independent manipulation of the proposed object.

In ordinary text, prosodic focus is often absent or represented through typography, clefts, lexical particles, or construction-specific devices. Changing those cues also changes salience, syntax, or lexical content. Conversely, directly constructing candidate alternatives and patching a latent direction risks assuming the very object the paper claims to discover.

A positive result would therefore compress too easily to `a constructed focus-related latent feature can steer focus-sensitive outputs`; a negative result would be hard to distinguish from a bad instrument. The cross-operator ambition does not fix that identification problem.

**Do not reopen by:** capitalization/bold markers, another focus particle, another operator, a larger handcrafted candidate set, or another patching/SAE method.

**Reopen only if:** there is a natural operator-independent focus manipulation with a published behavioral mother phenomenon, plus an intervention whose semantics is defined without first learning/choosing the latent direction from the target effect.
