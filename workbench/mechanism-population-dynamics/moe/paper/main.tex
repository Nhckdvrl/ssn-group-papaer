\begin{abstract}
What does learning to select expert subsets establish about the division of computation in a mixture-of-experts model?
We distinguish three relationships: executing a useful subset inside a trained model, transporting a predictive property with a parameter package, and reusing that package's realized computation in another context.
Across mature, architecture-matched public training paths, better selective execution coexists with stronger dependence on shared computation at similar intact quality.
Source interventions identify routed functions that transport a low-budget likelihood advantage without transporting intact quality.
On a fixed panel of 55 additional subjects, this advantage persists after clamping realized routes, while the removal cost of unchanged shared functions also increases.
Thus, these effects cannot be explained solely by the routing changes induced by the transported functions.
Cross-context replay of actual shared messages then reveals a prediction cost that largely survives local norm and projection correction.
In a second mature lineage without shared experts, ordinary annealing produces an opposite trend: native restricted damage increases while the annealed expert bank improves restricted likelihood in the parent receiver. Thus, native deployment curves can conceal transportable component properties. Together, the results characterize selectable computation through its learned production and use relationships, rather than through expert identity alone.
The transported advantage concerns probability quality; an accuracy advantage remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question: what becomes modular?}
Sparse activation makes a large function bank computationally affordable, but does not by itself specify how that bank divides a task.
Training can constrain expert selection, and selective execution can retain useful task performance~\cite{emo}.
The scientific question is what kind of computational organization this success identifies.
Does a successful subset behave as an independently reusable collection of functions, or does its utility depend on relationships with the rest of the learned system?

This question is compatible with cooperation being useful for deployment.
Interaction-aware pruning already exploits cooperative structure~\cite{hope}, and retaining shared computation can preserve substantial performance during condensation~\cite{cdmoe}.
Our target is therefore not a generic claim that experts interact.
We ask how learning history changes the relationship between \emph{selection}, \emph{property transport}, and \emph{predictive dependence}, and use interventions to locate where these relationships are implemented.

The evidence supports three connected statements.
First, improved selective execution can coexist with greater reliance on shared computation.
Second, the parameter package carrying the selective-execution advantage need not be the partner whose native deletion is most damaging.
Third, unchanged functions---and even their realized messages---can have different predictive roles in different computational contexts.
The realized-route intervention connects the second and third statements by excluding a routing-only explanation within the controlled receiver.

\section{Objects, interventions, and evidence}
We study four public mature paths from the EMO release~\cite{emo}: a standard pre-annealing parent $P$, standard annealing $A$, EMO annealing $B$, and full-course EMO $F$.
These are architecture-matched public histories, not a verified population of independent initializations or an isolated timing treatment.
Let $D$ denote backbone, attention and readout, $G$ the gate parameters, $R$ the routed expert functions, and $S$ the always-active shared functions.
An $R/S$ intervention covers the corresponding suites in all 16 layers; it is not evidence for a smallest single-expert unit.

For an intact loss $L$, we measure selection by the loss increase under a calibration-defined expert pool, alongside fixed random pools of the same size.
We measure property transport by changing a parameter source while holding a common receiver fixed.
We measure a function's predictive role by its removal effect in the specified system.
These are operationally different questions, not alternative names for one modularity score.

Gate parameters held fixed do not imply realized routing held fixed: changed expert outputs can change later hidden states and gate outputs.
We therefore capture actual gate logits before the common pool mask and replay them while preserving the original softmax, top-$k$, multiplication, and accumulation program.
This clamps both support and weights.
If $z$ denotes a replayed trajectory, the shared role in that condition is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_S(R,z)=L(D_P,G_P,R,0;z)-L(D_P,G_P,R,S_P;z).
\end{equation}
Other responses remain endogenous.
These interventions do not identify an isolated expert direct effect or a unique mediation percentage.

Language-model measurements use 64 windows and 20 file clusters.
The initial task panel contains 128 biology and mathematics questions.
The route experiment adds a fixed 440-question panel: eight questions in each of the other 55 MMLU subjects, with separate validation calibration.
The panels use different calibration sample counts, so between-panel magnitude differences cannot be assigned solely to task content.
We report answer-token NLL, choice CE, accuracy, and output KL separately.
Confidence intervals are paired input resampling intervals conditional on the models; they are not training-seed intervals.

\section{Three results and their connection}
\subsection{Selection improves while shared dependence grows}
The late- and full-course EMO endpoints $B/F$ have intact text NLL $2.6218/2.6212$ and identical task raw accuracy $39.06\%$.
Yet $F$ has lower selected-pool damage, while removing shared computation adds $1.8509$ more text NLL damage than in $B$ (95\% CI $[1.3311,2.3010]$).
The corresponding task answer-NLL difference is $5.3112$; the raw-accuracy damage difference remains unresolved.
Random-pool controls show that general robustness and useful selection coexist.
The result is a measured coexistence of selectability and cooperation, not an assertion that cooperation obstructs selection (Figure~\ref{fig:native}). On the previously specified set of 40 questions answered correctly by all four endpoints, both suites are jointly necessary in 8/10/20/24 cases for P/A/B/F. These are selected-set counts, reported alongside the full panel without population confidence claims.

\subsection{A property carrier also changes the role of an unchanged partner}
In the common $P$ receiver, replacing $R_P$ by $R_B$ transfers a low-budget answer-NLL advantage on the original panel, whereas replacing shared functions alone does not transfer the same property.
Full-quality transport fails: the original-panel full NLL increases $0.0802$ while low-budget NLL decreases $0.1882$.
Holding $S_P$ fixed and changing $R$ increases its removal effect by $0.3386$.
The function package that carries a useful operating property and the native necessary partner must therefore be identified separately.

The larger subject panel strengthens this statement and locates its runtime implementation (Figure~\ref{fig:routes}).
The natural low-budget NLL advantage is $0.4207$ ($[0.3221,0.5234]$).
At fixed $P/B$ routes, the routed-function source effect remains an improvement of $0.2937/0.3824$, while the unchanged shared suite's removal cost increases $0.2891/0.1840$; all four intervals exclude zero.
Routing still matters: the $B$ trajectory improves $R_B$ by a further $0.1270$, and the function--trajectory interaction is $-0.0887$ ($[-0.1242,-0.0500]$).
The data support neither a routing-only explanation nor route-independent autonomous components.

The choice CE advantage is $1.1386$ ($[0.8431,1.4676]$), but the raw accuracy difference is $+1.14$ percentage points ($[-2.27,4.55]$).
Importantly, shared dependence here is measured in the full regime and the transported advantage in the low-budget regime.
Their coexistence does not yet establish shared compensation as the cause of the budget advantage.

\subsection{Realized messages retain context-dependent value}
Function identity leaves production and use entangled.
We therefore fix shared functions and cross their actual messages between routed-function contexts on the same input.
Foreign messages have an average answer-NLL cost of $0.2605$ relative to their original pairing.
Correcting the consumer's clean norm and residual projection reduces this cost only to $0.2459$ ($[0.2096,0.2819]$).
The improvement is $0.0146$ ($[0.0050,0.0250]$), concentrated in biology rather than mathematics; choice CE and accuracy do not show equivalent recovery (Figure~\ref{fig:messages}).
The tested local scalar interface does not account for most of the prediction mismatch.
This is not a proof against richer learned alignment, nor does arbitrary direction destruction identify semantic content.

\section{Position in the literature and next discriminating test}
Expert specialization, cooperation, and reusable features have substantive precedents.
EMO establishes a training route to selective deployment~\cite{emo}; HOPE demonstrates a deployment benefit from retaining cooperative expert structure~\cite{hope}; CD-MoE demonstrates effective condensation that retains shared computation~\cite{cdmoe}.
Spectral decoupling changes parameter and gradient sharing and improves training~\cite{sdmoe}; its common matrices are not the same object as a replayed shared-expert message.
Basic--refinement attribution already supplies real shared/routed ablations~\cite{basic}.
Stitching provides genuine functional-feature transport after alignment~\cite{stitch}, while direct and invariance-aware matching clarify what successful alignment establishes~\cite{howstitch,invariance}.
Our contribution is the combined identification of \emph{which function package carries a deployment property}, \emph{how an unchanged partner's role changes}, and \emph{which realized routes and message interfaces account for these effects} in mature MoE histories.

The appropriate scale is a causal study of these relationships in mature systems, not a universal theory of single-expert semantic identities.
\subsection{A second lineage reveals an opposite native trend}
We evaluate OLMoE's public 5.033T checkpoint and its ordinary 100B annealing endpoint~\cite{olmoe}, crossing backbone, gate and expert sources under full computation and one fixed parent-calibrated pool of 16/64 experts.
This architecture has no always-active shared expert; we do not invent one.
All 26 predeclared conditions cover the same 55 subjects and 440 question texts, re-encoded by the OLMoE tokenizer.

After annealing, native restricted NLL damage increases by $0.1050$ ($[0.0021,0.2059]$).
Yet the annealed expert bank improves restricted NLL in the common parent receiver by $0.2230$ at fixed parent routes ($[0.1459,0.3021]$), with a choice-CE improvement of $0.4876$ ($[0.0844,0.8817]$).
Backbone changes oppose this property: at fixed parent gate parameters, changing only the backbone increases restricted NLL by $0.3575$ with the parent bank and $0.3176$ with the annealed bank (Figure~\ref{fig:lineage}).
Native deployment curves therefore need not monotonically track a component's property in a controlled receiver.

The restricted regime is severely impaired: raw accuracy is only $22.05/21.14\%$ compared to intact $43.64/43.18\%$.
We identify a probability-quality property, not a deployable compressed model, and do not change the pool to rescue performance.
This extends the source--context separation to another mature lineage; it does not replicate all shared-message claims in an architecture without shared experts.
Different tokenizers also preclude directly subtracting the two lineages' absolute NLL values.

\paragraph{The next explanatory comparison.}
A compact rival is output-gain calibration: expert-source changes may adjust routed amplitude while the surrounding system compensates.
Prior work establishes real global-conditioning effects from massive activations and super experts~\cite{massive,super,melayer}; self-selection based on internal activation is also a substantive training approach~\cite{aoe}.
Neither a transferred likelihood advantage nor local shared-message correction by itself excludes a gain account of the routed property.
The next registered comparison therefore fits one positive RMS-matching factor per layer on fixed calibration responses, without optimizing predictive loss, then evaluates parent, donor and gain-adjusted parent under identical realized routes in both lineages.
For EMO, shared removal is also evaluated in the restricted regime to test whether that online partner is required for the advantage.
No result from this comparison is included here.

\section{Traceability and remaining scope}
All plots copy existing audited point estimates and original confidence intervals; their sidecar gives JSON paths and SHA256 for every value.
The complete grids, reverse directions, accuracy results, failed attempts and numerical-source packages remain available.
Identity replays, causal-prefix checks, source read-only checks and independent formula recomputation validate the measurement program; they do not substitute for an independent training population.
The native formation trajectories, smallest reusable units, and the scope across mature lineages remain questions to investigate.
This argument is anchored in observed separations and controlled pathways, without requiring them to be universal or treating a nearby method as a reason to abandon the scientific question.
