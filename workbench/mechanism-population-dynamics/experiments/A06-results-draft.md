## 2 A natural nature × nurture experiment

**Suites.** *DataDecide* (Magnusson et al., 2025) pretrains OLMo-architecture models at 14 sizes (4M–1B parameters) on 25 corpus recipes — C4, DCLM variants, Dolma 1.6 / 1.7 and ablations, Falcon RefinedWeb variants, FineWeb-Edu / -Pro and their mixtures — each with three seeds. For each size we use the recipes on which all three seeds start from the same step-0 weights (verified from the checkpoint hashes; 13–25 recipes per size) and the latest step shared by all of them. At 1B we use the full 3-seed × 25-recipe crossing at the final step (E35) and a 5-seed × 10-recipe crossing at step 7500 (E45). *Pythia* (Biderman et al., 2023) trains a standard and a deduplicated model at each size from the same initialization; we verified tensor-level identity of the step-0 weights at 70M, 160M, 410M, 1.4B, 2.8B (exact), 1B (rounding-level, correlation 0.99993), and 6.9B and 12B (identical files). PolyPythias (van der Wal et al., 2025) provides nine independently initialized seeds at 70M–410M as the different-seed reference. Because a published step-0 checkpoint need not be the state a run actually started from, we also check the earliest training checkpoints; this audit reveals that PolyPythias' "weight-seed" variants were in fact trained from the standard initialization (Appendix A).

**Pairs.** A crossing yields three kinds of model pairs: same seed, different corpus (SI); same corpus, different seed (SD); and both different (DD). A property is *seed-determined* if SI pairs agree more than SD and DD pairs, *data-determined* if SD pairs agree more than SI and DD, and *universal* if all pairs agree.

**Head-role maps.** For each model we compute a [layer × head] map per role from attention patterns on fixed probe material: induction (attention from the second copy of a random 128-token block to the token after the first occurrence), previous-token (attention to t − 1 on natural text), attention sink (attention to position 0), retrieval (attention from the answer position to the context entity in knowledge-conflict prompts), duplicate-token, current-token, two-back and delimiter heads, and — as a measure that involves no attention pattern — the OV copying score of each head computed from its weights (Elhage et al., 2021). Maps computed on natural text are reliable within a model (split-half 0.99–1.00).

**Separating "which layer" from "which head".** A role map mixes two questions: which layers host a role, and which head within a layer takes it. We measure the first by the Spearman correlation of the per-layer role profiles and the second by the within-layer Spearman correlation of head scores, averaged over layers. Heads within a layer are exchangeable under the architecture's permutation symmetry, so any agreement in *which head* is a statement about symmetry breaking.

**Lemma 1 (the data cannot choose the head).** Let a layer have H heads, let π permute them (permuting the corresponding query, key, value and output blocks), and assume (i) the initialization distribution is invariant under π (true for i.i.d. per-parameter initialization), (ii) training is π-equivariant — the update applied to π·θ is π applied to the update of θ (true for gradient descent, Adam/AdamW, weight decay and global-norm clipping, because the loss is π-invariant) — and (iii) the training data and batch order are independent of the initialization. Let w(θ) be the head with the largest score for a role, with scores computed from the network's internals, so that w(π·θ) = π(w(θ)). Then for every corpus D, P(w(θ_T) = h | D) = 1/H, and for two independently initialized models trained on any corpora D, D′, P(w = w′) = 1/H and the expected within-layer rank correlation of their score vectors is exactly 0.
*Proof sketch.* By (i)–(ii), θ_T and π·θ_T have the same distribution given D, so w(θ_T) is uniform over heads. Independent initializations make w and w′ independent, so they agree with probability Σ_h (1/H)² = 1/H; the same exchangeability argument averages any rank correlation to zero. ∎
*Consequence.* "Which head" can never be chosen by the data: different-seed agreement is at chance by theorem (we observe −0.02 to 0.00 for nine roles), and any above-chance agreement between models trained on different corpora is carried by their shared initialization. The empirical question is therefore whether a seed's choice *survives* a change of corpus (inheritance) or is re-drawn by each corpus (scrambling) — which is what the rest of the paper measures.

**Statistics.** We report recipe-bootstrap confidence intervals for SI − SD, unbiased two-way variance components (seed, data, residual) for every head and every behavioural metric, permutation tests with recipe labels permuted (Mantel tests for pair-level regressions), and pre-specified role thresholds (an induction map is used only when the model has an induction head, max score > 0.3).

**The determination map** (Fig. 2) summarizes, for thirteen properties of the mechanism — from the algorithm to the weight values — which of the four components carries it, with the experiment and statistic behind each entry.


## 3 Nature decides where — and the weights forget it

**Placement is inherited, and the data contributes nothing to it.** In the 1B crossing (3 seeds × 25 corpora, E35), two models grown from the same seed on different corpora agree on which head within a layer takes each role (within-layer Spearman 0.27–0.34 for induction, previous-token and retrieval maps), whereas two models grown on the same corpus from different seeds agree no more than chance (≈ 0.00). An unbiased two-way variance decomposition of every head's rank gives a data component of zero; the seed effect is significant in 56–80% of heads, and the data effect in 5–7%, the false-positive rate. The same holds with five seeds at 1B (within-layer 0.32 / 0.47 / 0.29) and for corpora that share no sources. Concretely, for one seed, head 12 of layer 7 is the strongest induction head in 14 of 25 corpora and head 13 of layer 12 the strongest retrieval head in 13 of 25 (chance: 1.6 of 25; Fig. 1).

**Across scale, families, roles and measurements.** Inheritance is present at all 14 DataDecide sizes and grows monotonically with model size (Spearman with log parameters 0.81–0.92; Fig. 4a): it is weak up to 20M (within-layer 0.01–0.11), rises to 0.17–0.30 at 150M–300M and reaches 0.27–0.47 at 530M–1B. In the second family, Pythia's standard and deduplicated models — identical step-0 weights, Pile vs. deduplicated Pile — agree on head identity (within-layer 0.49–0.59 at 70M–410M) far above every pair of independently initialized PolyPythias seeds (≈ 0), and the inheritance continues all the way to 12B (1B 0.56–0.72, 6.9B 0.49–0.60, 12B 0.38–0.51, each against a permutation 95th percentile of 0.04–0.16; E58). The result is not specific to the three roles: across all 75 1B models, duplicate-token, current-token, two-back, delimiter and sink heads are all seed-placed (within-layer 0.16–0.36 vs. −0.02–0.00 for different seeds; every CI excludes zero; E59), and so is a purely weight-based measure that involves no attention pattern at all, the OV copying score of Elhage et al. (0.21 vs. −0.01). Ablation-defined role maps are inherited in the same direction, though more weakly, as single-head ablations are blunted by redundancy (E42).

**The seed can be read off the layout** (E61, Fig. 4b). Given a model trained on a corpus never paired with its seed, we compare its head layout with sibling models trained on other corpora and pick the seed whose siblings it resembles most. From 60M parameters upward this recovers the seed with 98–100% accuracy (chance 33%; 100% with five seeds at 1B, chance 20%); in Pythia, the true sibling ranks first among ten candidates at every size. SeedPrints (2026) identifies a weight lineage from output biases; the anatomy identifies independently trained siblings, although their weights retain a correlation of only 0.03–0.09 with the initialization. The layout is identifiable already at 3–4% of training (100% at 60M–300M; E61 on E57 checkpoints).

**What is not seed-specific.** The layer that hosts each role is shared by all models regardless of seed or corpus (layer-profile similarity 0.5–0.8 for both same-seed and different-seed pairs), and every model implements in-context copying with the same algorithm: a previous-token head K-composing with an induction head, above the 95th percentile of a random-head null in 75 / 75 1B models (E55). The seed therefore decides *which* of the exchangeable heads in a layer takes a role — nothing more and nothing less.

**Coordinates, not content.** The inheritance extends to the residual stream's coordinate system: same-seed models agree on which residual dimensions carry which variance (per-dimension correlation 0.20 vs. 0.00) and which dimensions carry massive activations (0.17 vs. 0.00). It does not extend to content. Measures that are invariant to the choice of units — CKA and best-match similarity — are no higher for same-seed than for unrelated pairs and are highest for models trained on the same corpus; MLP neuron identity is not inherited (0.008); and same-seed models are not linearly connected (interpolation barrier 7.5 vs. 7.0 nats for unrelated models; E43, Fig. 3).

**The weights forget the seed; the anatomy remembers it.** By the end of training, the correlation between final and initial weights is 0.03–0.09, same-seed models trained on different corpora are separated by a loss barrier as high as unrelated models, and their representations are no more similar than unrelated ones (§3). Our models are trained with AdamW, the optimizer family that erases the functional memory of initialization (forgetting-time view, 2026), and their behaviour indeed carries no seed main effect (§4). Yet the layout remembers the seed well enough to identify it (§7.1). What survives training is not a weight value or a function but a discrete choice among symmetric alternatives, made in the critical period and defended for the rest of training.

## 4 Nurture decides what

**Strength and timing.** How strongly a circuit is expressed is set by the data (significant data effect on 4 of 6 strength metrics, seed component ≈ 0; E35). When it forms is too: in controlled pretraining with six seeds and three corpora, the corpus explains 81% of the variance in the onset of induction heads and the seed none (p = 0.0001 vs. 0.47; E54), while previous-token heads form at the same time under every seed and corpus. This matches the observation that the data modulates the rate at which interpretable heads emerge (Chen et al., 2026) — the data controls *whether* and *when*, the seed *where*.

**No lucky seeds.** Across 11 benchmarks × 14 model sizes, the seed has a significant main effect in 3.9% (accuracy) and 7.8% (per-byte logit) of the 154 cells — the false-positive rate — while the corpus is significant in 75% and 98% (median variance components: seed 0.000, corpus 0.49–0.83; E51, Fig. 7). The same holds for context-vs-memory behaviour (0 / 12 conditions; E36). All seed variance in behaviour is seed × data interaction: a seed that is lucky on one corpus is not lucky on another, so seed selection cannot be transferred across recipes, and sharing seeds across recipes does not reduce the noise of recipe comparisons.

**A 1% slice of the data writes a behaviour.** Removing the ~1–2% of Flan instruction data from the Dolma recipe lowers the model's trust in a counterfactual context by 1.3–3.5 nats — but only when the question is posed in QA format; declarative prompts and the model's parametric knowledge are unaffected (C04). The switch is keyed to the literal template: "Question:" triggers it, the semantically equivalent "Q:" (+0.39) and "Query:" (+0.52) do not. It appears by 3.6% of pretraining and grows to +2.4 nats, it is present at 6 of 7 sizes from 60M to 1B, it replicates on PopQA and (partly) NQ-Swap, and in an independent training stack, OLMo 2 mid-training on a mix containing FLAN raises it from ≈ 0 to +2.1 nats. The switch is acquired during pretraining rather than by late exposure at the same dose: continued pretraining of a Flan-free 60M model on ~90M Flan tokens — about the exposure at which the switch appears in from-scratch training — does not install it (+0.04 ± 0.04 nats; E48b), whereas OLMo 2 acquired it during mid-training at a dose of billions of tokens. Unlike the basic roles of §3, the heads that implement the switch are stable within a model (split-half 0.93) but not placed by the seed (0.11 vs. 0.02): functions installed late by specific data are hosted flexibly.

## 5 Nature is written in a critical period

**Public suites lock in early.** In DataDecide 1B, same-seed agreement reaches its final level by 3.6% of training (E37); in Pythia-70M, by about 2% (E40). No single-head statistic of the initial weights or the initial gradient predicts which head will win (E39, E41): the slot is decided collectively during training, not read off the initialization.

**Controlled pretraining reveals a critical period** (E46, Fig. 5). We train small Llama-style models and branch them at step k (of 10,000) by switching the corpus to code or by adding Gaussian noise to every weight. Before the critical period closes, either intervention re-draws the slot: switching to code at step 100 (1%) leaves within-layer similarity to the unbranched run at −0.13 / 0.24, and noise as large as the weights at step 0 leaves 0.05. After it closes, the slot is an attractor: from step 250 (2.5%) onward, noise as large as the weights themselves returns roles to their slots (0.79–0.90 at step 250, 0.93–0.98 from step 500, the level of an exact rerun), and a switch to code preserves most of the layout (0.54–0.97, increasing with k). At initialization the basin is finite: perturbations up to 1% of the weight scale leave the layout unchanged (0.91–0.99), 10% partly re-draw it. The critical period replicates in a 30× larger controlled model (12 layers × 12 heads): switching to code at step 100 retains 0.32 / 0.48 of the layout, at step 1000 0.79 / 0.88, against 0.01 / −0.07 for the same seed trained on code from scratch.

## 6 How much is nature: SGD temperature and corpus statistics

**SGD temperature decides between seed and data** (E62, Fig. 6c). At fixed model size we vary the batch (16, 64, 512 sequences) and the learning rate (3e-3, 1e-3, 3e-4) and read the layout at step 2000, well after the critical period. The colder the training — the smaller the learning rate per token of batch — the more the seed alone decides the slot: two runs from the same seed on the same corpus that differ only in the order of their batches agree 0.43 at batch 16, 0.61 at batch 64 and 0.90 at batch 512 (an exact rerun: 0.91–1.00), and across all five recipes this agreement is perfectly ordered by temperature, and same-seed inheritance across c4 and scientific papers rises from 0.01 to 0.21–0.22, where it saturates at the ceiling set by the corpus distance. Tripling the learning rate has the effect of quartering the batch (0.07 inheritance, 0.57 order-only), so the ratio matters, not either factor. The DataDecide ladder spans the same temperatures — from 2.1e-7 learning rate per batch token at 4M to 1.5e-9 at 1B — and inheritance rises along it (Fig. 6c, right). In the suite, size and temperature are collinear; the controlled experiments separate them: at fixed temperature a 30× larger model inherits no more, at fixed size a colder recipe inherits more. Pythia, trained with 1024-sequence batches at every size, inherits strongly already at 70M.

**A corpus-distance law.** If the slot is chosen by symmetry breaking during the critical period, two corpora should yield the same slot to the extent that they present the same early-training statistics. Across DataDecide's recipe pairs, the Jensen–Shannon distance between the corpora's unigram distributions predicts how faithfully one seed's layout is reproduced on both, at every model size (E60, Fig. 6): Spearman −0.2 to −0.3 at 60M, −0.4 to −0.5 at 90M, −0.66 to −0.78 at 300M–750M, and −0.56 to −0.83 at 1B (Mantel permutation p ≤ 0.003 from 90M). The law holds unchanged on recipe pairs that share no data sources (−0.36 to −0.82) and after partialling out source overlap, so it reflects token statistics, not shared documents — and specifically content-word statistics, what the corpora are about: function-word distributions barely differ across English corpora and add nothing once content words are accounted for (partial ρ for content words −0.24 to −0.78; E64). All DataDecide recipes are English web text (JS ≤ 0.06); beyond that range, controlled pretraining shows the same law in the extreme: c4 and scientific papers (JS 0.13) share the slot (induction 0.19 ± 0.06 over 9 seeds), whereas any pair involving source code (JS 0.40–0.43) re-draws it (≈ 0; E46, E56). Pythia's standard and deduplicated Pile, nearly identical in their statistics, show the strongest inheritance of all, even at 70M (0.59).

**Larger models are more innate.** Inheritance grows with model size (Fig. 3a; Spearman with log parameters 0.81–0.92), the corpus-distance law sharpens with model size (Fig. 5b), and both are in place from the earliest checkpoints: at 10M–60M, same-seed agreement at 4–9% of training already equals its final value — small models never inherit, rather than inheriting and forgetting — while at 150M–300M inheritance is complete by 3% of training (0.30–0.40, slightly above its final 0.17–0.37) and the seed is identifiable from the layout at that point (100%; E57, E61). What makes larger models more innate is the temperature at which they are trained: at fixed hyperparameters a 30× larger controlled model inherits no more than the small one (c4–papers 0.14 vs 0.19–0.24; E46b), whereas lowering the temperature at fixed size raises inheritance (§6). Along the DataDecide ladder the batch grows from 32 to 704 sequences and the learning rate falls from 1.4e-2 to 2.1e-3, so the learning rate per batch token falls 140-fold, and inheritance rises along it (0.02–0.11 at the hottest, 0.27–0.47 at the coldest); Pythia, trained with 1024-sequence batches at every size, is innate already at 70M.

## 7 Applications

**Interpretability, attribution and benchmarking.** (i) Component-level findings transfer across data within a seed, not across seeds: ablating the ten most important heads found in a same-seed sibling (trained on another corpus) does 19.5% of the damage of ablating the model's own top ten, against 2.0% for heads found in a different-seed model trained on the *same* corpus and 10% for random heads from the same layers (E42). Report component findings per seed, and treat roles and algorithms as the reproducible units. (ii) Data attribution can explain the emergence of a component (Chen et al., 2026) but not its location; data-ablation and model-diffing studies should compare seed-matched models, or the seed's layout will masquerade as a data effect. (iii) No seed is lucky across recipes.

**A birth certificate.** Because the layout identifies the seed, it can certify that two models are siblings — independently trained from one initialization — complementing output fingerprints that track a single weight lineage (SeedPrints, 2026).

## 8 Discussion

**A protomap for language models.** Developmental neuroscience long debated whether cortical areas are specified by an intrinsic prepattern (the *protomap*; Rakic, 1988) or by the input they receive (the *protocortex*; O'Leary, 1989); the modern answer is that both contribute, with intrinsic gradients setting where areas form and input refining what they compute. Our results draw the same division inside language models. The random initialization acts as a protomap: it fixes, within a brief critical period, which of many interchangeable heads will host each function. The data acts as the protocortex: it decides which functions exist, how strong they are, when they appear and what they compute. Unlike a biological prepattern, the protomap of a language model is not readable at birth — no single-head statistic predicts the winner — and is written collectively by the first percent of training.

**Why the seed matters so precisely and so little.** Exchangeable heads make placement a symmetry-breaking event. Symmetry breaking is sensitive to the initial condition, to the forces acting at the moment of breaking — here the low-order statistics of the data — and to the noise present at that moment, here the SGD temperature; it is insensitive to everything that comes later. With hot SGD the particular early batches pick the winner; with cold SGD the seed does. This single picture accounts for the five quantitative facts in §3–§6: placement is inherited (same initial condition), only within the range of similar early statistics (same forces), more strongly the colder the training (less noise), it is decided early and then defended (critical period, attractor), and it says nothing about function (the weights and the behaviour forget the seed).

**What this changes in practice.** A circuit diagram of one model is a statement about one seed; its roles and algorithm generalize, its component labels do not, except to siblings trained from the same seed on similar data. Studies that compare models trained on different data — data attribution, model diffing, data-mixture ablations, the very DataDecide comparisons we build on — inherit a hidden confound when seeds are not matched, and a hidden opportunity when they are: seed-matched siblings share an anatomy, so component-level differences between them are attributable to the data.

**Limitations as scope.** Our primary measurements are attention-defined role maps; ablation maps and a weight-only copying score agree but are noisier, as single-head causal effects are diluted by redundancy. DataDecide sizes differ in more than parameter count, so the scale trend is a property of the suite's scaling recipe; the controlled models that isolate the critical period are small. The behavioural switch is studied for one instruction-data family, and a causal template-swap intervention by continued pretraining did not install it at the dose we could afford.
