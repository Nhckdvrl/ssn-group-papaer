## 1 Introduction

Mechanistic interpretability describes trained language models in terms of components: an *induction head* in layer 5, a *previous-token head* feeding it, a set of *retrieval heads* that copy facts from the context. Such descriptions are routinely read as properties of what the model learned from its data. But every trained model is the product of two independent random draws: its training data and its initial weights. Two models trained on the same corpus from different seeds put their induction heads on different units (Bali et al., 2026; van der Wal et al., 2025), and two models trained on different corpora differ in what they know and how they behave. Because existing studies vary one factor at a time, nobody knows which parts of a model's mechanistic anatomy come from which draw — whether a circuit's location, its strength, its timing and the behaviour it supports are *nature* (the seed) or *nurture* (the data).

We answer this question with a natural experiment hidden in public pretraining suites. DataDecide (Magnusson et al., 2025) trained the same three random initializations on up to 25 different corpora at each of 14 sizes from 4M to 1B parameters, and two further initializations at 1B; Pythia (Biderman et al., 2023) trained its standard and deduplicated models from identical initial weights at sizes from 70M to 12B. Crossing seeds with corpora lets us decompose every mechanistic property into a universal part, a seed part, a data part and a seed × data interaction. We audit the crossing from checkpoint hashes and from the earliest training checkpoints, and find in passing that one widely used seed-variation suite did not actually vary its initialization.

The decomposition reveals a clean division of labour (Fig. 2), which we summarize as **the seed picks the slot, the data fills it**:

- **What is universal.** Every model implements in-context copying with the same algorithm, a previous-token head composing with an induction head (75/75 models), and puts each role in the same layers regardless of seed or corpus.
- **What the seed decides.** *Which* head within a layer takes each role, and which residual-stream coordinates carry the outlier features, is inherited from the initialization. Models grown from one seed on different corpora pick the same previous-token head five times more often than chance, and the data's contribution to placement is zero. Inheritance holds across two model families and grows with scale, from 4M to 1B parameters [P: and to 12B in Pythia].
- **What the data decides.** Everything else: what the model represents, how strong its circuits are, when they emerge (the corpus explains 81% of the variance in induction-head onset; the seed explains none), and how the model behaves. Across 154 benchmark × size cells the seed has no main effect: there are no lucky seeds. A 1% slice of instruction data installs a switch that makes the model trust its context only when a question is introduced by the literal template "Question:".

Two further findings explain how the seed can matter so precisely and so little. First, placement is decided in a **critical period**. In controlled pretraining, changing the corpus or adding noise in the first 1% of training re-draws which head takes which role; from 2.5% of training onwards, even adding noise as large as the weights themselves returns the roles to their slots (similarity to the unperturbed run 0.79–0.98, against 0.05 for the same noise at initialization). Public suites lock in at the same point (2–4% of training). Second, **the weights forget the seed but the roles remember it**: final weights retain almost none of their initial values (r = 0.04), same-seed models are not linearly connected, and same-seed models represent their inputs no more similarly than unrelated models do — yet they agree on which head does what. What the seed leaves behind is a choice among symmetric alternatives, made early and then defended for the rest of training.

The slot is not inherited unconditionally. Corpora with very different low-order statistics — natural language versus source code — break the symmetry differently during the critical period, so the slot is re-drawn [P: E60 quantifies this as a dose–response on corpus distance]; and small models inherit weakly, because their early symmetry breaking is driven by the particular batches they see rather than by the seed [P: E46b controlled scale test; E57 developmental trajectories]. The larger the model, the more of its anatomy is fixed at birth.

These results have direct consequences. Component-level mechanistic claims are claims about a seed: they transfer to models grown from the same seed on other data, not to models grown from other seeds on the same data. Data-attribution and model-diffing studies should compare seed-matched models, or seed differences will masquerade as data effects. Seed selection does not transfer across training recipes, and sharing seeds across recipes does not reduce the noise of recipe comparisons. And the units of interpretability that reproduce across training runs are roles and algorithms, not components.

**Contributions.**
1. A natural factorial for mechanisms: seed × data crossings in DataDecide (14 sizes) and Pythia (70M–12B), with audits of the shared initialization.
2. The determination map: a decomposition of twelve mechanistic properties into universal, seed, data and interaction components.
3. Evidence that the seed picks the slot — across sizes, families, roles [P: E59] and measurement modalities — while the weights forget the seed.
4. Evidence that the data fills it — strength, timing, content, benchmarks and an instruction-template switch.
5. A critical period and basin of attraction for placement in controlled pretraining, with boundary conditions in scale and corpus statistics.
