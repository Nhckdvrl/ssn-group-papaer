## 1 Introduction

Take two language models grown from the same random seed, one trained on web text, the other on a different corpus. By the end of pretraining their weights retain a correlation of only 0.04 with the initialization, and across 154 benchmark × model-size cells the seed has no detectable effect on what they can do. Yet their induction heads, previous-token heads and retrieval heads sit on the same units — so reliably that a model's seed can be read off its circuit layout alone with 98–100% accuracy. **The weights forget the seed; the circuits remember it.**

Every trained model is the product of two random draws, its initialization and its data, and we do not know which parts of its mechanisms come from which. Mechanistic interpretability credits circuits to the data, and recent work traces individual heads to the training examples that formed them (Chen et al., 2026). Training is thought to erase the initialization, and adaptive optimizers do erase its functional imprint (forgetting-time view, 2026). Variation across seeds is treated as noise. Because stability studies vary only the seed (Bali et al., 2026; van der Wal et al., 2025; Tigges et al., 2024) and data studies vary only the data, nature and nurture have never been separated. We separate them using seed × corpus crossings hidden in public pretraining suites — DataDecide at 14 sizes from 4M to 1B parameters and Pythia from 70M to 12B — part of a broader shift from studying finished models to studying the training processes that produce them (Biderman et al., 2026).

Language models, it turns out, have a nature and a nurture. **Nature decides where.** Which head takes each role is inherited from the seed, identically across corpora; the data contributes nothing, and by the permutation symmetry of heads it provably cannot. **Nurture decides what.** How strong circuits are, when they emerge, what the model represents and how it behaves come from the data — there are no lucky seeds — and a 1% slice of instruction data is enough to write a specific behaviour: trusting the context only when a question is introduced by the literal template "Question:". **Nature is written in a critical period.** In controlled pretraining the slot is chosen during the first 1–2.5% of training — before then, switching the corpus or adding noise re-draws it; afterwards, even noise as large as the weights returns the roles to their slots. **Two levers set how much of a model is nature**: the temperature of SGD and the statistics of its corpus. Lowering the ratio of learning rate to batch size makes the layout insensitive even to the order of training batches (agreement 0.43 → 0.90), and the closer two corpora's token distributions, the more faithfully a seed's layout recurs on both. Because standard scaling recipes train larger models colder, **larger models are more innate.**

Our findings:
- **Nature decides where (§3).** For all nine head roles we measure — including a copying score computed from the weights alone — placement is inherited from the seed at every one of 14 sizes and in both model families, the data contributes nothing (by measurement and by theorem), and the layout identifies the seed with 98–100% accuracy from 60M parameters on. The algorithm and the layer of each role are universal.
- **Weights forget, circuits remember (§3).** Final weights retain a correlation of 0.03–0.09 with the initialization, same-seed models are not linearly connected and share no more representational content than unrelated models — yet they share an anatomy and a coordinate system.
- **Nurture decides what (§4).** Strength, onset time, content, benchmarks and context-versus-memory behaviour are set by the data with no seed main effect; a 1% data slice writes a context-trust switch keyed to "Question:", across sizes from 60M to 1B and in two independent training stacks.
- **Nature is written in a critical period (§5)** of symmetry breaking during the first 1–2.5% of training, after which the slot is an attractor — in controlled models of two sizes and in the public suites.
- **SGD temperature and corpus statistics set how much is nature (§6)**, and the cooling of standard scaling recipes makes larger models more innate.

These findings matter wherever models are compared or explained (§7). Component-level findings travel with the seed, not with the data: ablating a sibling's top heads does ten times more damage than ablating those of a different-seed model trained on the same corpus. Data attribution can explain whether a circuit forms but not where it lives. Model comparisons should be seed-matched. No seed is lucky across recipes. And a model's circuit layout is its birth certificate.

## Related-work positioning (one line each, for §8)
- **Stability across seeds** (Bali et al. ICML 2026; PolyPythias ICLR 2025; MultiBERTs ICLR 2022; Fehlauer et al. EMNLP 2025): vary only the seed. We cross seed with data and show the seed's effect is a heritable layout, not noise. Bali et al. find the residual stream more stable across seeds than the heads; our decomposition explains why: residual content is set by the data, head assignment by the seed.
- **Universality** (Chughtai ICML 2023; Gurnee TMLR 2024; Tigges NeurIPS 2024): algorithms stable, components variable. We explain why components vary (seed) and when they are shared (same seed, similar data).
- **Data → mechanisms** (Chan NeurIPS 2022; From Shortcut to Induction Head NeurIPS 2025; MDA ICML 2026): data shapes which algorithm forms and how fast. MDA finds that the samples driving one induction head drive the others too, and that data interventions change the rate of emergence but not the eventual outcome — with the seed held fixed. Our results complete the picture: the data acts on the mechanism, the seed decides which unit carries it.
- **Initialization memory** (SeedPrints ICLR 2026; forgetting-time view 2026; LMC, Frankle ICML 2020): the seed leaves output fingerprints; Adam-family training erases functional memory. We show an anatomical memory that survives both data change and weight forgetting; like the functional memory studied there, it is erased by hot SGD (large learning rate, small batch) and kept by cold SGD.
- **Training dynamics as the object of study** (Biderman et al. 2026 position paper; per-seed emergence forecasting 2026): we give a case where a lasting structural property is decided in the first 1–2.5% of training and is predictable from the seed; emergence *timing* across corpora is set by the data (E54), complementing per-seed forecasts within one corpus.
- **Critical periods and symmetry breaking** (Achille ICLR 2019; Critical periods in SSL ICLR 2026; multi-pathway winner-take-all ICML 2026; Pre-carved Niches 2026): we locate a critical period for *which component* takes a role in LM pretraining.
