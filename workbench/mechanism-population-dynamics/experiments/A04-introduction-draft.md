## 1 Introduction

Mechanistic interpretability describes trained language models in terms of components: an *induction head* in layer 7, the *previous-token head* that feeds it, a set of *retrieval heads* that copy facts from the context (Elhage et al., 2021; Olsson et al., 2022). Such descriptions are routinely read as properties of what the model learned from its data, and recent work traces individual heads back to the training examples that produced them (Chen et al., 2026). But every trained model is the outcome of two independent random draws: its training data and its initial weights. Studies of stability vary only the seed and find that the components behind a circuit change from run to run (Bali et al., 2026; van der Wal et al., 2025; Tigges et al., 2024); studies of data vary only the data. Because the two have never been varied together, we do not know which parts of a model's mechanistic anatomy are *nature*, fixed by the seed, and which are *nurture*, learned from the data. Answering this is part of a broader shift from studying finished models to studying the training processes that produce them (Biderman et al., 2026).

We separate them with a natural experiment hidden in public pretraining suites. DataDecide (Magnusson et al., 2025) trained the same random initializations on up to 25 different corpora at each of 14 sizes from 4M to 1B parameters; Pythia (Biderman et al., 2023) trained its standard and deduplicated models from identical initial weights at every size from 70M to 12B. Crossing seeds with corpora decomposes every mechanistic property into a universal part, a seed part, a data part and an idiosyncratic remainder (Fig. 2).

The decomposition reveals a division of labour that we summarize as **the seed picks the slot, the data fills it** (Fig. 1). Our findings:

- **A natural nature × nurture experiment for mechanisms (§2).** We identify and audit the seed × data crossings hidden in DataDecide and Pythia — finding along the way that one widely used seed-variation suite did not in fact vary its initialization — and organize thirteen mechanistic properties into a *determination map*.
- **The seed picks the slot (§3).** Which head within a layer becomes an induction, previous-token or retrieval head is inherited from the initialization, whatever the corpus. The data's contribution to placement is zero; for a given seed, a single head is the layer's strongest in up to 56% of 25 corpora (chance: 6%). This holds at all 14 DataDecide sizes and in Pythia [P: up to 12B], [P: for every head role we measured, including a purely weight-based copying score]. What does not depend on the seed is equally clear: every model uses the same algorithm, a previous-token head composing with an induction head (75/75 models), and puts each role in the same layers.
- **The data fills it (§4).** Everything else comes from the data: how strong circuits are, when they emerge (the corpus explains 81% of the variance in induction-head onset, the seed none), what the model represents, and how it behaves. The seed has no main effect on any of 154 benchmark × size cells — there are no lucky seeds — and a 1% slice of instruction data installs a switch that makes the model trust its context only when a question is introduced by the literal template "Question:".
- **A critical period, then an attractor (§5).** In controlled pretraining, changing the corpus or adding noise during the first 1% of training re-draws which head takes which role; from 2.5% of training on, even noise as large as the weights themselves returns every role to its slot. By the end of training the weights retain almost nothing of their initialization (r = 0.04) and same-seed models are not linearly connected — **the weights forget the seed, but the anatomy remembers it.**
- **Inheritance follows corpus statistics and scale (§6).** The more similar two corpora's token statistics, the more faithfully a seed's layout is reproduced on both: across DataDecide's recipe pairs the corpus distance predicts layout agreement at every model size (reaching Spearman −0.8 in the largest models), also for corpora that share no sources; a corpus as different as source code re-draws the slot. The law sharpens with scale, and so does inheritance itself: the larger the model, the more of its anatomy is fixed at birth.

These findings have practical consequences (§7). A model's head layout alone identifies its seed: from 60M parameters upward, a leave-one-corpus-out classifier recovers the seed of a model trained on an unseen corpus with 98–100% accuracy, and the true sibling ranks first among ten Pythia candidates — although the trained weights retain a correlation of only 0.04 with their initial values. Component-level interpretability findings transfer between models that share a seed, not between models that share data; data attribution should attribute the *emergence* of a component to data but not its *location*; model-diffing and data-ablation studies should compare seed-matched models; and seed selection cannot be carried across training recipes. More broadly, the reproducible objects of interpretability across training runs are roles and algorithms; which component carries them is an accident of birth.

## Related-work positioning (one line each, for §8)
- **Stability across seeds** (Bali et al. ICML 2026; PolyPythias ICLR 2025; MultiBERTs ICLR 2022; Fehlauer et al. EMNLP 2025): vary only the seed. We cross seed with data and show the seed's effect is a heritable layout, not noise.
- **Universality** (Chughtai ICML 2023; Gurnee TMLR 2024; Tigges NeurIPS 2024): algorithms stable, components variable. We explain why components vary (seed) and when they are shared (same seed, similar data).
- **Data → mechanisms** (Chan NeurIPS 2022; From Shortcut to Induction Head NeurIPS 2025; MDA ICML 2026): data shapes which algorithm forms and how fast. We show the data does not choose the component.
- **Initialization memory** (SeedPrints ICLR 2026; forgetting-time view 2026; LMC, Frankle ICML 2020): the seed leaves output fingerprints; Adam-family training erases functional memory. We show an anatomical memory that survives both data change and weight forgetting.
- **Training dynamics as the object of study** (Biderman et al. 2026 position paper; per-seed emergence forecasting 2026): we give a case where a lasting structural property is decided in the first 1–2.5% of training and is predictable from the seed; emergence *timing* across corpora is set by the data (E54), complementing per-seed forecasts within one corpus.
- **Critical periods and symmetry breaking** (Achille ICLR 2019; Critical periods in SSL ICLR 2026; multi-pathway winner-take-all ICML 2026; Pre-carved Niches 2026): we locate a critical period for *which component* takes a role in LM pretraining.
